# 06 · How to Start Selling Cloth (Suit Rolls) — The Complete Guide

> *"How do we start selling cloth rolls like suit cloth to customers? We don't have that for now."*

This file answers exactly that. It is written for someone who has **never bought a metre of fabric
for resale** and wants to end up running a serious fabric business in Jalandhar.

---

# PART A — Understand the business you're entering

## A1. The fabric value chain and where you can sit

```
Fibre (cotton / polyester / viscose / wool)
   ↓
Yarn spinning            → Punjab: Ludhiana, Mandi Gobindgarh, Malerkotla; also Gujarat, Haryana
   ↓
Weaving / knitting       → Ludhiana (knits), Surat (synthetics), Bhilwara, Coimbatore, Panipat
   ↓
Dyeing & finishing       → Ludhiana, Panipat, Surat; Punjab has a dedicated Dyeing & Finishing policy
   ↓
MILL / BRAND             → Raymond, Arvind, Vimal, Siyaram's, Blackbuck, Bombay Dyeing,
                           Trident, Reid & Taylor, plus hundreds of unbranded Ludhiana/Surat mills
   ↓
DISTRIBUTOR / C&F AGENT  → state-level; takes 5–9% margin
   ↓
WHOLESALER / STOCKIST    → city-level (Ludhiana Gandhi Nagar, Delhi Gandhi Nagar); 8–15%
   ↓
RETAILER (per metre)     → your shop; 30–50% gross margin  ◄── YOU START HERE
   ↓
CONSUMER / TAILOR
```

**Key insight:** the **retailer captures the largest single margin in the chain** (30–50% vs
8–15% for a wholesaler) because he does the work nobody else will do — he holds 200 designs in
stock, lets a customer touch them, measures and cuts 3.5 m, and advises on colour.

Your unfair advantage over a normal fabric retailer: **you also stitch.** So you convert fabric
into a finished product and take the stitching margin too. A fabric shop's conversion rate is
maybe 25%; yours can be 60%+ because the customer has no reason to walk to another tailor.

## A2. The three ways to sell cloth — choose your starting point

| Model | Capital | Margin | Risk | Verdict for you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Swatch-book selling (zero stock)** | ₹3,000–15,000 | 20–30% | Almost zero | **Start here — literally this week** |
| **2. Retail counter (rolls by the metre)** | ₹4–10 L | 30–50% | Dead stock | Your Stage B, in Jan–Feb 2027 |
| **3. Wholesale/distribution (rolls in bulk to trade)** | ₹20–60 L | 10–18% | Credit + volume | Your Stage C, year 2–3 |

**Model 1 in detail — how to sell fabric you don't own:**
1. Buy 2–4 **swatch books / hanging sample books** from Ludhiana wholesalers (₹300–1,500 each;
   some are free with a first order). Each holds 40–120 designs.
2. Show the customer. They choose design **X**.
3. You take **50–100% advance** and write a fabric order against it.
4. Next morning you drive to Ludhiana (50 km, ~1 hour) and buy exactly 3.5 m + 10% wastage.
5. You stitch. Your margin: retail ₹399/m vs landed ₹230/m = **₹592 per suit length**, zero
   inventory, zero unsold rolls.
6. After 2–3 months you'll know exactly which 30 designs sell. **Then** you buy stock — of only
   those 30 designs. This is how you build a ₹5 L stock without a single rupee of guesswork.

> Do Model 1 for 8–12 weeks. It is the market research you would otherwise pay for in dead stock.

## A3. What "cloth roll" actually means (trade vocabulary you must learn)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Than / thaan / roll** | A full factory roll. Usually **30–60 m** (some 100 m+). Buying "per than" is cheaper than per metre. |
| **Cut piece / lot** | Short lengths (3–20 m) from mill overruns or export rejects — **cheap, irregular, high margin, high risk**. Sold by weight or per piece in Delhi's Chandni Chowk/Katra Neel and Surat. |
| **Suit length** | A packed 3.2–3.8 m piece with lining, sold as a unit. Brands sell boxed "suit lengths" at a premium. |
| **GSM** | Grams per square metre — fabric weight. Shirting 100–140 GSM; suiting 220–280 GSM; winter suiting 280–340+ GSM. |
| **Count** | Yarn fineness (e.g. 2/60s, 2/100s). Higher count = finer yarn = smoother, costlier, more delicate. |
| **PV / poly-viscose** | The workhorse Indian suiting blend. Durable, cheap, holds a crease, slightly shiny. |
| **TR / terry rayon** | A cheaper suiting alternative; softer drape, less durability. |
| **Poly-wool / wool blend** | 30–70% wool. Winter premium segment. |
| **100% wool / pure wool** | Top end; Super 100s/120s/150s refers to yarn fineness. |
| **High-twist** | Tightly twisted yarn → crease-resistant, matte, drapes well. The modern formal look. |
| **4-way stretch / lycra suiting** | Trending fast in India for slim-fit suits; ₹350–700/m wholesale. |
| **Selvedge** | The woven edge. Branded fabric has the brand name printed along it — **this is how you prove authenticity**. |
| **Shrinkage** | % a fabric contracts on washing/steaming. Cotton 3–8%; PV 1–2%. Add it to your cut. |
| **Nap / pile** | Directional texture (velvet, corduroy) — all pieces must be cut the same way. |
| **Bemberg / cupro lining** | The premium lining. Selling Bemberg instead of polyester lining is a ₹200 upgrade with ₹600 of perceived value. |
| **Canvas / haircloth** | The chest piece inside a jacket. **Full canvas** = hand-stitched horsehair (expensive, drapes beautifully, lasts decades). **Half canvas** = fused top + canvas chest. **Fused** = glued interlining (cheap, bubbles after 3 years). This is the single biggest quality differentiator you can talk about. |
| **MOQ** | Minimum order quantity — usually per than (30–60 m) or 100 m at mills. |
| **HSN codes you'll use** | 5208/5210 (cotton woven fabric), 5407 (synthetic filament woven), 5515 (other synthetic staple woven), 5111/5112 (woollen worsted fabric), 5401–5406 (yarn/filament) |
| **SAC for stitching** | 9988 (tailoring / job work) |

## B2. The colour rule

**30% of your stock value must sit in seven shades: navy, charcoal, black, mid-grey, beige/khaki,
olive, and brown.** These never die, they cross seasons, and they're what 8 out of 10 first-time
suit buyers choose. Fashion colours (burgundy, rust, bottle green, powder blue) are **10% of stock
maximum** and get cleared within one season.

## B3. What NOT to stock in year 1

- ❌ Sarees (different customer, different skill, huge capital, brutal competition)
- ❌ Anything over ₹1,200/m at retail until you have 200 customers who trust you
- ❌ More than 40 m of any single design
- ❌ Cut pieces / lot fabric until you can grade quality by touch (they're where beginners get cheated)
- ❌ Ladies' lehenga fabric beyond 5–6 designs — deep capital, slow turn
- ❌ Fabric you personally don't like. You'll never sell it convincingly.

# PART C — Where to buy (sourcing map)

## C1. Ludhiana — your primary source (50 km, ~1 hr by road)

Ludhiana is India's woolen-knitwear and hosiery capital (Punjab makes ~95% of India's woollen
knitwear) and has a dense suiting/shirting wholesale trade. **Go here first, always.**

**Areas to cover in a 2-day trip:**

| Area | What's there | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gandhi Nagar Market / Ganesh Market / Hukam Singh Road** | Ludhiana's biggest garment + fabric wholesale cluster; hosiery, shirting, suiting, ready-made | Thousands of shops; many are manufacturers with their own labels. Best for shirting, knits, ready-made. |
| **Chaura Bazar / Ghas Mandi / Ghora Gali / Dr. Gujjarmal Road** | Traditional textile & suiting-shirting wholesalers | Old-city lanes; parking is a nightmare; go early (9–11 am); prices are sharpest here |
| **Field Ganj / near Jama Masjid** | Textile wholesalers, suiting | |
| **Mali Ganj / Dal Bazar** | Knitwear wholesalers | |
| **Focal Point / Bhamia Road** | Manufacturers, export houses, hosiery, industrial | Where you go later for bulk/OEM and private label |
| **Model Town / Sarabha Nagar (retail)** | Branded showrooms — Raymond, Arvind, Vimal etc. | **Not for buying.** Go to *study* their display, rate cards and pitch. |

**How to work a Ludhiana trip (2 days, ~₹6,000):**
- **Day 1, 9 am – 6 pm:** walk 20–25 shops. In each: introduce yourself as a **Jalandhar retailer
  starting a fabric counter + tailoring studio**, ask for the **rate list and MOQ**, photograph the
  designs (with permission), collect visiting cards, and ask three questions:
  1. *"Per than rate kya hai, aur per metre rate kya?"*
  2. *"Kitna credit doge, aur kis terms pe?"*
  3. *"Agar shade match na ho ya defect nikle, replacement kaise hoga?"*
- **Day 1 evening:** sit in a hotel, build a spreadsheet: supplier · design · composition · GSM ·
  width · per-m rate · per-than rate · MOQ · credit · replacement policy · GST number.
- **Day 2:** revisit your top 5 and **negotiate**. Then buy **samples + one trial than** from 2–3.
- **Always get a GST invoice.** Without it you lose your input tax credit (5% on fabric) and you
  can't prove stock value to a bank.

**Negotiation levers that actually work:**
- Pay **cash within 7 days** for a 2–3% discount.
- Buy a **full than** instead of cut metres: typically 5–12% cheaper.
- Commit to a **monthly quantity** in exchange for a fixed rate for the season.
- Take the **last 10 m of a roll** ("ending pieces") at 20–40% off — perfect for your own
  stitching and for sample books.
- Ask for **free sample/hanging books** with your first order — most wholesalers will give 2–5.

# PART D — Buying: quality, negotiation, and not getting cheated

## D1. Quality inspection at the time of purchase (learn these 8 tests)

| Test | How | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Selvedge check** | Look for the brand/composition print along the woven edge | Genuine branded fabric vs a "Raymond-type" copy |
| **2. Composition claim vs feel** | Crush a handful for 10 seconds, release. PV springs back with creases; cotton stays creased; wool recovers slowly and smoothly | Whether the "wool blend" is actually wool |
| **3. Burn test (ask permission, use a thread)** | Cotton: burns to grey ash, smells of paper. Polyester: melts into a hard bead, smells chemical. Wool: self-extinguishes, smells of burnt hair, crumbly ash | The single most reliable composition test. Do it once per new supplier. |
| **4. GSM / weight** | Weigh a 10×10 cm swatch × 100 = GSM (a ₹600 digital scale does this) | Whether you're paying 280 GSM money for 220 GSM cloth |
| **5. Shade continuity** | Unroll 3–5 m; compare against the sample in daylight, not tube light | Shade variation ("shade patti") is the #1 cause of a ruined suit — two legs of a trouser in different shades |
| **6. Width** | Measure with a tape. Claimed 58"/60" is common; 44" fabric needs more metres for a suit | A 44" roll can add 0.5 m to every suit = ₹200 loss per order if you quoted 58" maths |
| **7. Defect scan** | Run the roll across a table under bright light looking for slubs, holes, oil stains, uneven dyeing, skew | Reject or claim a discount on the spot |
| **8. Shrinkage** | Ask the supplier for the shrinkage %, or steam/press a swatch and re-measure | Determines how much extra you must cut |

## D2. Supplier due-diligence checklist (before the first big order)

- [ ] GST number verified on the GST portal
- [ ] Sample physically approved and **kept, labelled, dated, signed by both parties**
- [ ] Written rate list with validity dates
- [ ] MOQ, per-than vs per-metre rates, and mixed-design than allowance
- [ ] Replacement / rejection policy in writing (what happens on shade variation, defects, shortage)
- [ ] Credit limit and days, and the cash-discount alternative
- [ ] Delivery: who pays freight, how long, and do they deliver to Jalandhar
- [ ] At least one reference from another retailer (call them!)
- [ ] A test order of **one than** before a ₹2 L order

## D3. Negotiation script (use it verbatim, in Punjabi/Hindi)

> "Main Jalandhar ton aa, nava fabric counter kholl reya haan te naal made-to-measure studio vi hai.
> Pehli vaar main sirf ik than lavanga, par je rate te quality theek hoyi tan main har mahine
> 300–500 metre lavanga, saal bhar. Tussi mainu **opening rate** daso — distributor wala, retail
> wala nahi. Te cash 7 din vich de dawan, ohde te kinni chhoot doge?"

Then: **be silent**. Let them fill the silence.

Follow with: *"Do hor dukaan te eh rate miliya hai"* (have a real competing quote in your pocket).
And: *"Mainu 3 designs chahide ne, har design da ek than — je ikattha do tan rate ki banega?"*

**Targets for a first order:** 8–15% below the quoted retail-wholesale rate, 2–3% cash discount,
free sample books, free or 50% freight, and a written 15-day replacement promise.

## D4. Payment and paperwork discipline

- Never pay 100% advance to a new supplier. Standard: 30–50% advance, balance on delivery/against
  documents, or full cash-on-pickup for the first 3 orders.
- **GST invoice with your GSTIN printed on it** — without it you lose 5% ITC and your stock value
  isn't provable to a bank.
- Keep every invoice in a month-wise file. Fabric stock is your loan collateral in year 2; the
  invoices are what makes it collateral.
- Pay on time, always, in year 1. Your **reputation as a payer** is what unlocks 30-day credit and
  the best rates in year 2. In Punjab's textile trade, credit terms follow payment behaviour, not
  order size.

## E3. Merchandising — how a fabric shop actually sells

1. **The roll wall is your billboard.** Height order: bestsellers and high-margin at **eye and
   hand level (4–5.5 ft)**; slow movers at the top; heavy/cheap at the bottom.
2. **Group by use, not by colour:** "Formal Suiting" · "Winter Wool Blends" · "Shirting" ·
   "Kurta & Pathani" · "Trousering" · "Wedding & Sherwani" · "Lining & Trims" · "Ladies".
   Customers think in occasions.
3. **Every roll gets a visible ticket:** design code · composition · GSM · width · **price/metre** ·
   price per suit length. Shops that hide prices lose 40% of walk-ins who are too shy to ask.
4. **Pull-out hangers:** 40 hero designs on wooden hangers showing a 1-m drape, with a photo of a
   finished garment stitched from it. **This single display converts browsers into buyers.**
5. **The "finished garment next to the fabric" rule:** hang one of your stitched suits next to the
   fabric it was made from, with the total price. *"Eh suit isi cloth ton baniya — kul ₹6,500."*
   No cloth shop in Jalandhar can do this. Only you can.
6. **Mannequins:** 2 gents (one in a suit, one in a Pathani/kurta) + 1 ladies. Change every season.
7. **Lighting test:** a fabric that looks dull under your lights will not sell. Bring a swatch home
   and check it in daylight before you decide the display lighting.
8. **Smell and sound:** fabric shops smell of dust and sizing. A ₹500 diffuser and clean floors
   change the perceived price by 20%.

# PART F — Selling fabric retail: the mechanics

## F1. Three price points, always displayed

| Presentation | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Per metre** | ₹399/m | The trade standard; comparability |
| **Per suit length (3.5 m + lining)** | ₹1,650 | Removes maths anxiety; raises ticket |
| **Cloth + stitching package** | ₹6,500 all-in | Your killer offer; nobody else can quote it |

## F2. The 10-step sale (train your salesman on this)

1. Greet in 10 seconds. *"Ki banauna hai ji — suit, shirt ya kurta?"*
2. Ask the **occasion** and the **date**. (Date tells you if you can even take the order.)
3. Ask the **budget range** — out loud, comfortably: *"Budget kive rakhna chahunde o, ₹2,000 tak
   ya ₹5,000 tak cloth?"*
4. Pull **3 rolls** in that range. Unroll 1 m of each. Drape it over their forearm.
5. Move to the **mirror**. Hold the fabric against their face and shoulder. Say what you see:
   *"Tuhade rang te eh navy wadhiya lagega, eh grey thoda flat lagu."*
6. **Educate once:** composition, GSM, brand, why this costs more. 30 seconds. Then stop.
7. Quote the **suit-length pack** price first, then per-metre.
8. **Attach:** lining, buttons, pocketing, interlining, thread. ("Eh Bemberg lining laa do,
   suit 5 saal chalega" — +₹450.)
9. **Attach stitching:** *"Assi hi sil ke de dinde haan. 10 din. Pehli fitting 5ve din. Rate
   ₹X, te aaj hi naap ho javega."*
10. Bill it, pack it, ask for the phone number, add to the database, request the Google review.

**Conversion targets:** 55% of walk-ins buy fabric; **35% of fabric buyers also book stitching**;
average bill ₹1,800 (fabric only) → ₹5,500 (with stitching).

## F3. Staffing and incentives

- **1 fabric salesman** (₹12,000–18,000 + **1% of fabric revenue** or ₹15 per metre above target)
- **1 cutter/measurer** (₹10,000–14,000) — accurate measuring is a *retention* issue; a short cut
  is a permanent customer loss
- Train both on: composition, GSM, the burn test, colour matching to skin tone, the 10-step sale,
  and GST billing.
- **Never let a tailor quote fabric prices** and never let the salesman quote stitching. One voice
  per product.

## F4. Billing, GST and compliance for fabric retail

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Woven fabric (HSN 5208, 5210, 5407, 5515, 5111/5112) | **5%** GST |
| Garments / made-ups **up to ₹2,500 per piece** | **5%** |
| Garments / made-ups **above ₹2,500 per piece** | **18%** |
| Tailoring / stitching service (SAC 9988) | **5%** (reduced from 18% under GST 2.0, effective 22 Sep 2025) |
| Fashion design + tailoring bundled as a design service | possibly **18%** — ask your CA |

**Critical operational points:**
1. **The ₹2,500 test is PER PIECE, not per bill.** A bill with 4 shirts at ₹1,800 each is 5% on
   all four. A single suit at ₹8,000 is 18% on that piece.
2. **Fabric + stitching on one invoice = a composite/mixed supply question.** The cleanest
   structure most small businesses use: **two line items** — fabric (goods, 5%) and stitching
   (service, 5%). Both are 5% today, so it's simple — but get your CA to confirm the invoicing
   format, because if the rate structure changes again this matters enormously.
3. **Take Input Tax Credit.** You pay 5% GST when you buy fabric and collect 5% when you sell it —
   with a GST invoice on both sides, your net GST outflow is only on the margin. **This alone is a
   reason to be GST-registered from day one.**
4. **Composition scheme:** for goods the limit is ₹1.5 Cr, for services ₹50 L. Composition blocks
   ITC and blocks B2B sales to GST-registered buyers — so **as soon as you sell wholesale to other
   GST-registered tailors, you must be in the regular scheme.**
5. **E-way bill** required for goods movement above ₹50,000 (interstate and, in many cases,
   intrastate) — you'll hit this constantly once you deliver rolls by van. Learn the portal.
6. Register on **IndiaMART/TradeIndia** as a seller once you wholesale — B2B buyers search there.
7. Keep a **stock register reconciled monthly** — GST officers and bank auditors both ask for it,
   and it's your theft control.

## G2. What the fabric counter needs to break even

From the model: at 20 m/day and ₹500/m with a 38% gross margin, the counter **just** breaks even
(₹4,000/month EBITDA). At **32 m/day** it earns ₹80,000/month (18% margin). At 12 m/day it loses
₹32,000/month.

> **A fabric shop is a traffic business.** Every rupee of rent is fixed; every metre is margin.
> Your entire marketing effort converts into metres-per-day, and metres-per-day converts into profit.
> **Minimum viable footfall: 25–30 m/day. Do not sign a lease for a shop whose location cannot
> plausibly deliver that.**

## G3. Capital cycle and stock turn

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Stock turn | **≥ 6×/year** (every 60 days) | Below 3× your capital is asleep |
| Stock days | ≤ 60 | |
| Working capital cycle | Pay supplier in 30 days, sell stock in 45 days | Negative cash cycle = the business funds itself |
| Receivables (retail) | 0 | Never |
| Receivables (wholesale) | ≤ 35 days | Beyond that you're a moneylender |
| Dead stock (>90 days) | < 8% of stock value | Above 15% → clearance sale immediately |
| Shrinkage/theft loss | < 1% | Above 2% → your measuring discipline is broken |

## G4. How a beginner actually loses money in fabric (read this twice)

| Failure | How it happens | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Dead stock** | Buying 40 designs × 30 m because "sab chalta hai" | Buy depth in 30 proven designs, not breadth in 100 |
| **Wrong season** | Wool in March, linen in October | Season calendar on the godown wall; buy 8 weeks ahead, clear 4 weeks early |
| **Shade variation** | Two thans of the "same" design in different shades; a customer's two trouser legs differ | Buy one than per design per order; keep the supplier's signed sample; match shade in daylight |
| **Under-measuring** | Cutting 3.4 m when you billed 3.5 m, every day, forever | Measuring machine calibrated monthly; bill the machine reading; spot-audit 5 cuts a week |
| **Unrecorded cutting** | Your own tailors taking cloth for stitching without a job card entry | **Every metre leaves the roll only against a job card.** Reconcile weekly. |
| **Discount spiral** | Competing with Sadar Bazar on price | Never compete on price; compete on the *package* (cloth + fit + guarantee) |
| **Credit to retail** | "Jaane-pehchaane ne, baad vich de jaange" | Zero credit at retail. Advance or UPI. No exceptions, no relatives. |
| **No GST invoice** | Cash sales, no records, no ITC, no bank loan later | Invoice 100% of sales from day one |
| **Fire/water loss** | No insurance on ₹8 L of fabric | Insure the stock from day one. Fabric burns in minutes. |
| **Buying on emotion** | You loved the design; the market doesn't | Buy what the top-20 selling designs tell you to buy |

# PART H — 30-day launch checklist for the fabric counter

**Days 1–7 — decide**
- [ ] Choose location; shortlist 3 shops; negotiate rent (target ≤ 4% of expected monthly revenue)
- [ ] Written business decision: categories, capital, break-even metres/day
- [ ] Bank current account + GST registration + Udyam
- [ ] CA engaged; billing software chosen (Tally Prime / Busy / Zoho Books)

- [ ] Ludhiana trip: 25 suppliers met, spreadsheet built, 5 shortlisted
- [ ] Delhi trip (or IndiaMART quotes) for price comparison
- [ ] Sample approval: signed, labelled, dated samples from each shortlisted supplier
- [ ] Trial order: 1 than each from 3 suppliers — inspect on arrival with the 8 tests
- [ ] Negotiated rate card + written credit/replacement terms

**Days 15–21 — build**
- [ ] Fit-out: racks, lighting, counter, measuring machine, cutting table, trial rooms, signage
- [ ] Fabric codes assigned to every roll; stock register opened
- [ ] Rate board printed; price tickets on every roll
- [ ] 40 hero designs on hangers; 2 finished garments displayed next to their fabric
- [ ] Staff hired and trained on the 10-step sale + GST billing
- [ ] CCTV + fire extinguisher + insurance policy in place

**Days 22–30 — launch**
- [ ] Soft launch to your customer database (10% off for old customers for 10 days)
- [ ] Grand opening on an auspicious date; invite the 10 vendor partners
- [ ] Instagram: 9 posts before launch day, then 3 reels/week
- [ ] Google Business Profile updated: "Fabric store" category + 15 new photos
- [ ] First 50 trade buyers visited with the swatch bag and wholesale rate card
- [ ] Day-1 to Day-30 tracking: metres/day, conversion %, average bill, attach rate

**The launch-day metric that matters:** if you are not at **15+ m/day by day 30**, the problem is
location or assortment — not marketing. Fix it before you add stock.
