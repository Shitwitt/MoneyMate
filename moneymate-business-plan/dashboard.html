Why this works — four structural advantages

1 · You take margin twice.
A cloth shop sells fabric at 35–45% and stops. A tailor sells stitching at 50% and stops. You sell a ₹6,500 "cloth + stitch" package that earns ₹3,645 (56%) — versus ₹1,800 for stitching only. Same customer, same visit, same tailor hours.
2 · Fabric is inventory; labour isn't.
Tailoring revenue stops when the shutter drops. Stocked fabric sells at 8 pm, sells on Sunday, and can move in 500-metre wholesale lots without hiring a single extra tailor.
3 · You supply your own competitors.
Every tailor in Doaba who buys cloth from you is a customer whose growth is your growth. That is a distribution moat a readymade showroom can never build.
4 · Punjab is the right state.
Ludhiana (50 km) makes ~95% of India's woollen knitwear. Punjab has ~1.46 lakh textile units, 10+ lakh textile workers, ~14% of state manufacturing GVA from textiles, ~₹12,241 Cr of textile exports, three dedicated textile policies, and an Industrial & Business Development Policy 2026 offering capital subsidy up to 20% of fixed investment.

The fatal move: opening a big cloth shop before you have a reputation for fit. Then you're just another fabric retailer competing on price with 200 shops in Sadar Bazar — and you'll discount stitching to move dead cloth, which trains customers to expect discounts forever. Fit first → price second → cloth third → marketing fourth → scale fifth.

How to Start Selling Cloth (Suit Rolls)

You don't sell cloth today. Here is the exact path from zero to a serious fabric business — starting with ₹3,000 and no inventory.

Step 0 — the three ways to sell cloth

ModelCapitalMarginRiskVerdict
1 · Swatch-book selling (zero stock)₹3–15k20–30% Almost noneStart this week
2 · Retail counter (rolls by the metre)₹4–10 L30–50% Dead stockStage B — Jan/Feb 2027
3 · Wholesale / distribution₹20–60 L10–18% Credit + volumeStage C — year 2–3
The swatch-book loop (this is the whole trick):
1. Buy 2–4 hanging sample books from Ludhiana (₹300–1,500 each; 40–120 designs each).
2. Customer picks design X → you take 50–100% advance → write a fabric order.
3. Next morning you drive 50 km to Ludhiana and buy exactly 3.5 m + 10%.
4. You stitch. Margin: ₹399 retail − ₹230 landed = ₹592 per suit length, zero inventory, zero dead rolls.
5. After 8–12 weeks you know exactly which 30 designs sell. Then you buy stock — of only those 30.
That is how you build ₹5 L of stock without a single rupee of guesswork.

Step 1 — learn the trade vocabulary

Than / rollA full factory roll, usually 30–60 m. Per-than rates are 5–12% below per-metre.
Cut piece / lotMill overruns & export rejects. Cheap, irregular, high margin — high risk. Don't touch until you can grade by feel.
Suit length3.2–3.8 m packed with lining. Sell it as a unit — it removes the customer's maths.
GSMWeight. Shirting 100–140 · suiting 220–280 · winter suiting 280–340+.
Width58–60" is standard. A 44" roll needs ~0.5 m more per suit — ₹200 loss per order if you quoted 58" maths.
PV / TRPoly-viscose (the Indian workhorse) / terry rayon (cheaper, softer, less durable).
High-twist · 4-way stretchCrease-resistant matte formal; stretch suiting is trending fast (₹350–700/m wholesale).
SelvedgeThe woven edge. Branded fabric prints the brand along it — your proof of authenticity.
ShrinkageCotton 3–8%, PV 1–2%. Add it to every cut.
Bemberg / cuproPremium lining. A ₹200 upgrade carrying ₹600 of perceived value.
CanvasThe chest piece. Full canvas = hand-stitched horsehair. Half = fused top + canvas chest. Fused = glue, bubbles in 3 years. The biggest quality story you can tell a customer.
MOQMinimum order — usually one than (30–60 m), 100 m at mills.
HSN5208/5210 cotton woven · 5407 synthetic filament · 5515 other synthetic · 5111/5112 woollen worsted.
SAC 9988Tailoring / job work — 5% GST.

Step 2 — your opening assortment (8 categories, ~1,290 m)

The colour rule: 30% of stock value must sit in seven shades — navy, charcoal, black, mid-grey, beige/khaki, olive, brown. Fashion colours max 10%, cleared within one season. Depth over breadth: 30 proven designs × 40 m beats 100 guesses × 12 m.

Step 4 — the 10-step fabric sale

1 Greet within 10 seconds. "Ki banauna hai ji — suit, shirt ya kurta?" 2 Ask the OCCASION and the DATE. (The date tells you if you can even take the order.) 3 Ask the BUDGET out loud, comfortably. "₹2,000 tak ya ₹5,000 tak cloth?" 4 Pull 3 ROLLS — not 30. Unroll 1 m of each. Drape it over their forearm. 5 Move to the MIRROR. Hold it against their face. Say what you see. 6 EDUCATE ONCE — composition, GSM, brand, why it costs more. 30 seconds. Then stop. 7 Quote the SUIT-LENGTH PACK price first, then per metre. Anchoring. 8 ATTACH — lining, pocketing, buttons, zip, interlining, thread. (55–70% margin.) 9 ATTACH STITCHING — "Assi hi sil ke de dinde haan. 10 din. Aaj hi naap ho javega." 10 BILL it, pack it, take the phone number, add to the database, ask for the review.

Targets: 55% of walk-ins buy fabric · 35% of fabric buyers also book stitching · average bill ₹1,800 (fabric only) → ₹5,500 (with stitching).

Step 5 — shop fit-out (₹3.7–7.5 L)

How beginners lose money in fabric — dead stock (buying 100 designs), wrong season (wool in March), shade variation, under-measuring every day forever, unrecorded cutting by your own tailors, discount wars with Sadar Bazar, credit to retail walk-ins, no GST invoice (losing 5% ITC), no fire insurance, and buying what you like instead of what sells.

Fabric price ladder

Three pricing rules that never break

1 · Never discount Signature/Bespoke.
Discount the scope instead — move them to Essential. Protects your price integrity forever.
2 · Raise prices every October, by 10–12%.
Announce it by WhatsApp with a "old rates till 31 Oct" deadline. A price rise becomes a booking surge.
3 · Rush fee 30–50%, always.
Wedding-season urgency is the highest-margin money you'll earn. Not charging it means subsidising bad planning.

ITC is free money: you pay 5% buying fabric in Ludhiana and collect 5% selling it. With GST invoices on both sides you pay tax only on your margin. That 5% is 25–30% of your gross margin. Being registered from day one pays for itself.

Shop Operations & SOPs

A tailoring business becomes a real business the day its quality stops depending on who is standing in the shop.

The order flow — one job card that never leaves the garment

Order book → Measurement card → JOB CARD created ↓ CUTTING (master cutter signs) ← fabric issued from stock (fabric code + metres + roll reading) ↓ STITCHING (tailor name + start/finish date) ↓ FITTING 1 (customer present — notes written in words AND numbers) ↓ CORRECTION → FITTING 2 → FINISHING (buttons, hem, thread trim) ↓ QC 11-POINT CHECKLIST (signed) → PRESS / STEAM → PACK (garment bag + care card + spare buttons) ↓ DELIVERY (customer signs · full payment · photo taken · review requested · next reminder set)

Three boards on your wall

Order board
Every live order as a card in columns: New · Cutting · Stitching · Fitting · Finishing · Ready · Delivered. Nothing hides.
Delivery calendar
The next 21 days with every delivery date. Anything red gets attention today.
Rework board
Every alteration, whose fault, why. Reviewed every Sunday. One tailor appearing 5 times is a training or salary issue — not a customer issue.

Measurement protocol

Men — full chart
Neck · Chest · Waist · Hip/Seat · Shoulder (bone to bone) · Sleeve length · Sleeve round/bicep · Wrist · Jacket length · Trouser length · Thigh · Knee · Bottom opening · Crotch/rise · Front rise · Back rise.

Plus posture: stooped / erect / sloping shoulder L-R / high-low hip / prominent seat / belly / chest.
Ladies — full chart
Bust · under-bust · waist · hip · shoulder · armhole (front/back) · sleeve length · sleeve round · bicep · wrist · kurta length · salwar length · neck depth front & back · bust point-to-point · dart length · dupatta length · lehenga length + waistband.

Plus figure notes: sloping shoulder, full bust, flat seat, sway back, maternity.
Rules: measure over thin clothing · tape parallel to the floor · two fingers' ease where ease is intended · measure both sides for shoulder and sleeve and record the larger · write cm and inches · never round to a convenient number · tell the customer "normal khade ho jao, saah chhadd ke" so they don't suck in.

One numbered card per customer → photographed → stored in a Sheet with phone, occasion, order history and a next reminder date. 500 customers × ₹6,000 × one repeat every 2 years = ₹15 L a year you never advertise for.

QC — 11 points, signed before packing

Every measurement matches the card within ±0.5 cm
No puckering or skipped stitches; SPI 10–12 shirts, 8–10 coats
Seams pressed open/flat and finished (overlock or bound)
Stripes/checks match at centre back, shoulders, sleeves, pockets
Lining smooth, correct colour, no twist, adequate ease
Buttons secure (shanked on coats), buttonholes clean, no fraying
Zip runs smoothly, right length and colour, no fabric caught
Hems even all round; trouser break correct at the shoe
No thread ends, chalk marks, pins, stains or iron shine
Final press crisp; collar & lapel roll correct; creases sharp
Label, care card, spare-button envelope, clean garment bag

Cutting discipline — where your fabric profit lives

  • Inspect before cutting: shade continuity, weaving defects, stains, and shrinkage (3% on 3.5 m = 10 cm lost).
  • Record the roll reading before and after on the job card. Stock theft in a fabric shop is almost always unrecorded cutting, not robbery.
  • Nap/pile direction — all pieces the same way for velvet and corduroy.
  • Match checks and stripes at centre back, shoulders, sleeves, pockets. A mismatched check loses a premium customer faster than anything.
  • Marker efficiency ≥ 85%. Every 5% saved on 1,000 m/month at ₹250/m = ₹12,500/month.
  • Keep end-pieces ≥ 30 cm separately — they become pocketing, facings, samples and free swatches for the trade network.
  • Two-person rule on bespoke: cutter cuts, owner verifies against the measurement card. Both sign.

Fittings — the trust moment

  • Always by appointment. A customer who waits 40 minutes at a fitting never books again.
  • Garment is basted, not finished — nothing cut away permanently at fitting 1.
  • Pin on the body, then write the changes on the job card in words and numbers, and photograph front/side/back.
  • Movement tests: "baaahein uthaa ke vekho, baith ke vekho" — a mirror doesn't catch these.
  • Never say "theek hai ji" while pinning. Say what you're changing and why — that is what justifies the Signature price.
  • Check in order: shoulder line → chest ease → waist suppression → sleeve pitch & length → collar hug (no gap) → jacket balance → trouser waist/seat → break at the shoe → all lengths.

Risks, KPIs and the 25 Mistakes

The four existential risks

1 · Trade receivables
Kills more Punjab textile businesses than anything else. A 15.5% wholesale margin is wiped out by two defaulters in ten. Mitigation: written credit policy before the first credit sale; slabs (₹25k/21d → ₹3L/45d); automatic supply stop at the limit; PDCs for Slab C/D; 2% cash discount for 7-day payment; 18% p.a. late interest invoiced and enforced at least once; 1.5% monthly bad-debt provision; and sales-boy incentive paid on collections, not sales.
2 · One-man dependency
No second cutter, no SOPs, no systems. Mitigation: train a second cutter from year 1; write every SOP; document pattern blocks digitally; a front-desk hire; one full day off per week from year 2; a written "if I'm unavailable for 30 days" plan.
3 · Family / ownership conflict
The most common killer of Punjabi family businesses. Mitigation: written roles, written profit share, a monthly family business meeting with minutes, and strictly separate personal and business accounts from day one.
4 · Dead stock + over-expansion together
You open a second shop to "move" the stock you couldn't sell in the first. Mitigation: the 6-months-profitable-without-you rule; max 40 m per design until it sells through once; a hard 90-day clearance rule.

The 25 mistakes

Pricing & positioning
  1. Competing on price instead of fit, service and guarantee.
  2. No printed price list — so every order is a negotiation and every negotiation a discount.
  3. No rush fee in wedding season.
  4. Discounting the premium tier instead of moving the customer down a tier.
  5. Charging the same for a Tuesday shirt and a groom's sherwani.
Fabric buying
  1. Buying 100 designs × 20 m instead of 30 designs × 40 m.
  2. Buying stock before 8 weeks of swatch-book selling.
  3. Not keeping a signed, dated sample of everything ordered.
  4. Ignoring width (44" vs 58").
  5. Paying cash without a GST invoice — losing 5% ITC forever.
Operations
  1. No job card, so nobody knows where a garment is.
  2. Booking 130% of capacity and delivering late in season.
  3. Letting tailors take cloth off rolls without recording it.
  4. No rework log — the same mistake repeats 40 times a year.
  5. Fittings by "call kar denge" instead of a booked appointment.
Money
  1. Business cash in the personal savings account.
  2. No salary for yourself, so you can't see real profit.
  3. No cash reserve going into July–September.
  4. Credit to retail customers.
  5. No written credit policy before the first wholesale credit sale.
Growth
  1. Opening a second shop before the first runs without you.
  2. Hiring a 4th tailor without raising the average charge per garment.
  3. No customer database — so every season starts from zero.
  4. Marketing as "posting on Facebook sometimes" instead of a weekly system.
  5. Building wholesale before the retail fit reputation exists.
Marketing gaps worth naming
  • No Google Business Profile with 45+ reviews — your highest-ROI free asset.
  • No fit-transformation reels — the highest-performing content format in this trade.
  • No WhatsApp database with +3-month and +9-month reminders.
  • No vendor-partner network (makeup artist, photographer, tent house, banquet, visa agent).
  • No NRI program — Doaba's single biggest unfair advantage.

The last page — five sentences to remember
