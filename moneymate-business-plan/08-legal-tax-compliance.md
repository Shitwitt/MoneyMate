# 08 · Legal, Tax, Licences, Schemes and Funding (Punjab / India, Nov 2026)

> **Read this once, then give it to a CA.** Everything here is accurate as at November 2026, but
> tax and scheme rules change and are applied by officers with discretion. Nothing in this file is
> legal or tax advice — it is a **shopping list of questions to ask a professional**, in the right
> order, so you don't get sold something you don't need.

---

## 1. Choose the legal structure

| Structure | Cost | Best when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | ~₹0 (just registrations) | Start here. One owner, simplest tax filing (business income on your ITR), full control | Unlimited personal liability; harder to raise equity; name isn't protected |
| **Partnership firm** (registered) | ₹3,000–8,000 | 2 family members with defined shares | Unlimited liability; disputes are fatal; register the deed |
| **LLP** | ₹8,000–15,000 + ₹5,000–10,000/yr compliance | 2+ partners, want limited liability, no equity investors planned | Annual MCA filings (Form 8 & 11); audit above thresholds |
| **Private Limited** | ₹10,000–25,000 + ₹20,000–40,000/yr compliance | You plan investors, big bank limits, export, or multiple branches | Mandatory audit regardless of turnover; stricter compliance; 2 directors; harder to take money out |

**Recommendation for you:** **sole proprietorship for years 1–2**, convert to a **Private Limited
(or LLP)** when you (a) need a large bank limit, (b) bring in a partner/investor, (c) start
wholesale across states, or (d) begin exporting. Conversion later is normal and not expensive.

> Name protection is separate from structure: **file a trademark** for your brand in Class 24
> (textiles/fabric) and Class 25 (clothing) as soon as the name is decided. Government fee is
> ₹4,500 per class for an individual/small enterprise (₹9,000 otherwise); a professional charges
> ₹2,000–6,000 more. You can start using **™** the day you file, **®** only after registration.

## 3. GST — the part that changed in September 2025 (this matters a lot to you)

Under the **"GST 2.0" rationalisation effective 22 September 2025**, apparel and textile taxation
moved to a simplified two-slab structure:

| What you sell | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tailoring / stitching service** (job work on customer's fabric), SAC **9988** | **5%** | **Reduced from 18%.** This is a big deal for you — it makes a GST-registered tailor studio price-competitive against unregistered shops |
| **Textile job work** (embroidery, weaving, knitting, printing etc.) | **5%** | Reduced from 18% |
| Fabric (woven cotton HSN 5208/5210, synthetic filament 5407, other synthetic staple 5515, woollen worsted 5111/5112) | **5%** | |
| Garments / made-ups / apparel **up to ₹2,500 per piece** | **5%** | The old ₹1,000 slab was raised to ₹2,500 |
| Garments / made-ups **above ₹2,500 per piece** | **18%** | Raised from 12%. **Per piece, not per bill** |
| Knitted/crocheted articles | **5%** | |
| Man-made fibre **yarn** | 18% | Relevant only if you go upstream |
| Raw cotton | 5% under **reverse charge** | Buyer pays |
| Garment accessories (belts, ties, scarves) | see current schedule | Ask CA |

**Registration thresholds (Punjab, a normal-category state):**
- **Goods:** ₹40 lakh aggregate turnover (₹20 lakh if you also supply services — confirm your mix with the CA)
- **Services:** ₹20 lakh
- **Compulsory regardless of threshold:** interstate supply, e-commerce supply, casual taxable person, etc.

**Composition scheme:**
- Goods (incl. restaurant): turnover limit **₹1.5 Cr**
- Services (incl. stitching/renting): **₹50 lakh**
- **Blocks Input Tax Credit and blocks B2B invoicing** — so the moment you sell wholesale to
  GST-registered tailors and shops, composition is wrong for you. **Go regular.**

**Input Tax Credit (ITC) — why registering early is free money:**
You pay 5% GST when you buy fabric from Ludhiana. You collect 5% when you sell it. With proper GST
invoices on both sides, you set off the input against the output and pay GST **only on your margin**.
An unregistered shop pays the 5% as a cost and can't recover it. **That 5% is 25–30% of your gross
margin — registration pays for itself.**

**The composite-supply question you must ask your CA:**
When you sell "cloth from my shop + I stitch it", is that (a) two supplies (goods @5% + service @5%)
or (b) one composite supply, and if so what's the principal supply? Today both legs are 5% so the
cash answer is identical — but **if you sell a stitched garment above ₹2,500/piece it may be 18%**.
Get the invoicing format settled **in writing** before the wedding season.

**Return calendar (regular taxpayer):**

| Return | Frequency | Due |
|---|---|---|
| GSTR-1 (outward supplies) | Monthly (or QRMP quarterly) | 11th of next month |
| IFF (QRMP scheme, B2B invoices in month 1 & 2) | Monthly | 13th |
| GSTR-3B (summary + payment) | Monthly (or quarterly under QRMP) | 20th (22nd/24th by state group) |
| GSTR-9 (annual) | Yearly | 31 December after FY end |
| GSTR-9C (reconciliation, above threshold) | Yearly | With GSTR-9 |

If turnover is up to ₹5 Cr you can opt for the **QRMP** scheme (quarterly return, monthly payment).

**E-way bill:** required for movement of goods above ₹50,000 in value — you'll hit this the first
time you deliver a bulk roll order or move stock to a godown. Learn `ewaybillgst.gov.in`.

**TDS under GST** applies to certain government/large contracts at 1%; **TDS under income tax**
(194C on contract payments, 194J on professional fees, salaries) applies once you make those payments.

## 4. Income tax — how to file this business

- **Proprietorship:** business income is your personal income; file **ITR-3** (or ITR-4 if you opt
  for presumptive taxation).
- **Presumptive taxation:** Sections **44AD** (business) and **44ADA** (profession) let you declare
  a flat percentage of turnover as profit instead of maintaining detailed books. **Eligibility,
  percentages and thresholds were amended in recent budgets — ask your CA whether your mix of
  goods + services qualifies, and at what rate.** This is often the cheapest legal route for a
  business your size, but it caps your ability to claim actual (higher) losses or to show very low
  margins, so it's a decision, not a default.
- **Pay yourself a salary on the books.** In a proprietorship it isn't a deductible expense, but
  writing it down forces you to see real profit. In a Pvt Ltd it is deductible and is usually more
  tax-efficient than dividends.
- **Depreciation:** sewing machines, furniture, computers and vehicles are depreciable assets. Keep
  purchase invoices for everything — a ₹6 L fit-out is ₹6 L of depreciation you can claim.
- **Advance tax:** if your annual tax liability exceeds ₹10,000, pay in instalments
  (15 Jun / 15 Sep / 15 Dec / 15 Mar). Missing these costs interest under 234B/234C.
- **Accounting software:** Tally Prime, Busy (very popular with Punjab traders), or Zoho Books.
  Busy and Tally both handle GST + e-way bills + fabric stock in metres, which generic software
  often does badly. **Insist on "stock in metres with roll-wise balance"** when you demo it.
- **Keep records 8 years.** Invoices, purchase bills, bank statements, rent agreement, salary sheets.

## 5. Labour and shop compliance once you have staff

| Law | Applies when | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Punjab Shops & Commercial Establishments Act** | Any shop | Register; display hours, holidays, name board; maintain a muster roll |
| **Payment of Wages / Minimum Wages Act** | Any employee | Pay at least Punjab's notified minimum wage for your skill category; pay by the 7th/10th of the month; written wage slips |
| **Contract Labour Act** | 20+ contract workers | Usually not you — but if you use a labour contractor for peak season, check |
| **EPF** | Commonly at 20+ employees | 12% employer + 12% employee on basic; register on `epfindia.gov.in` |
| **ESI** | Commonly at 10+ employees, wage ceiling applies | 3.25% employer + 0.75% employee; gives your workers medical cover — a real retention benefit |
| **Workmen's Compensation / Employee Compensation Act** | Any employee | A needle injury or a pressing-iron burn is a workplace injury. Take a **group personal accident policy** (₹1,000–3,000 per employee per year) — cheap and it saves you |
| **Maternity Benefit Act** | 10+ employees | 26 weeks paid leave |
| **POSH Act** | 10+ employees | Internal complaints committee; especially relevant for a boutique with female customers and staff |
| **Child Labour Act** | Always | **Never employ anyone under 14**, and 14–18 only in non-hazardous work with conditions. A "chhota helper" in a tailoring shop is a serious legal and reputational risk. Use a paid apprentice above 18 instead. |
| **Apprentices Act** | Optional | Registering apprentices gives you structured cheap-ish labour with government stipend sharing in some schemes |

## 6. Insurance — non-negotiable list

| Cover | Why | Indicative annual cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Shop/fire + stock insurance** | Fabric burns in minutes; ₹8–20 L of stock can vanish. Insure at **replacement value**, declare stock value honestly | 0.3–0.6% of sum insured |
| **Burglary/theft** | Rolls walk out of godowns | included above |
| **Public liability** | A customer slips, a mirror falls | ₹3,000–8,000 |
| **Group personal accident for staff** | Machine injuries | ₹1,000–3,000 per head |
| **Health insurance for staff** (optional but powerful) | Retention | ₹6,000–15,000 per head |
| **Money-in-transit** | Once your sales boys carry collections | ₹3,000–10,000 |
| **Marine/transit cargo** | For inter-state dispatch and export | per shipment |
| **Key-man / business interruption** | If your master cutter or you are the business | later |

## 7. Funding — schemes and how to actually get money

### 7.1 Central credit schemes

| Scheme | Limit | What it's for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PM Mudra Yojana (PMMY)** — Shishu | up to ₹50,000 | Tiny working capital | Nil processing fee |
| **Kishor** | ₹50,001–₹5,00,000 | Your Stage A/B start | Apply via `udyamimitra.in` or any bank branch |
| **Tarun** | ₹5,00,001–₹10,00,000 | Stage B/C expansion | First-time borrower cap |
| **Tarun Plus** | ₹10,00,001–₹20,00,000 | Scale-up | **Only for those who already repaid a Tarun loan** — so take and cleanly repay Kishor/Tarun first; the repayment record is the asset |
| **Stand-Up India** | ₹10 L–₹1 Cr | **SC/ST and women** entrepreneurs (greenfield) | Jalandhar has a large SC population — this is highly relevant; needs 25%+ own contribution typically |
| **PMEGP** | up to ₹50 L (services ₹20 L) | New manufacturing/service units | **25–35% subsidy**; requires a project report and KVIC/DIC processing; slower but genuinely subsidised |
| **CGTMSE** | guarantee cover | Collateral-free cover for the lender | Ask your bank to route your term loan through CGTMSE — it removes the "what security?" objection |
| **SIDBI / NCDC / Punjab State term loans** | varies | Machinery, godown | |
| **Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU)** | — | Micro enterprises | |
| **PM Vishwakarma** | ₹1 L + ₹2 L tranches | **Traditional artisans & craftspeople — tailors are explicitly a covered trade** | Stipend during training, toolkit incentive up to ₹15,000, collateral-free credit at concessional rate, marketing support. **You and your tailors should look at this first — it is designed exactly for this trade.** |
| **Skill schemes: Samarth / PMKVY / Punjab Skill Development Mission** | — | Trained tailors | Samarth is placement-oriented textile skilling; Punjab's share of it has historically been tiny (only ~759 of 2.57 lakh trainees as of Feb 2024), so becoming an **implementing partner** is an opening — you'd get subsidised training for your own workforce. A proposed **Samarth 2.0** (~₹25,000 Cr over 5 years) has been reported. |

### 7.2 Bank finance as you grow (what the bank actually wants)

| Facility | Purpose | Typical requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Term loan** | Machinery, fit-out, godown | Quotations, 25% margin money, CGTMSE cover |
| **Cash Credit (CC) / Overdraft (OD)** | **Fabric stock** | Stock statements monthly, 2 yrs ITR + GST returns, drawing power against stock & receivables |
| **Bill discounting** | Against institutional POs | Firm PO + buyer creditworthiness |
| **Letter of Credit (LC)** | Buying from mills at volume, and for export | Bank limit + margin |
| **Packing credit / pre-shipment** | Export orders | IEC + confirmed export order |

**How to be loan-ready (do these now, borrow later):**
1. 100% of sales through the bank/UPI — **no unaccounted cash**. Banks lend against turnover they
   can see. A shop showing ₹8 L turnover but living like ₹40 L gets nothing.
2. Two years of filed ITR + GST returns + audited financials.
3. Udyam + GSTIN + current account + a stock register.
4. A **project report** (the financial model in this plan is 70% of one).
5. A CIBIL score above 720 on the proprietor — pay off every personal EMI and credit card on time.
6. A branch manager who knows you by name. Visit monthly, deposit regularly, keep the balance moving.

### 7.3 Punjab state incentives (verify current terms via Invest Punjab / District Industries Centre)

- **Punjab Industrial & Business Development Policy 2026** — a flexible "menu" model: investors may
  choose up to 20 incentives; **capital subsidy up to 20% of fixed capital investment capped at
  ₹10 Cr**; **up to 75% reimbursement of net SGST**; **100% electricity duty exemption**; total
  incentives up to **125% of FCI capped at ₹500 Cr**; incentive period **10–15 years**;
  **Employment Generation Subsidy** with eligibility lowered to **₹25 Cr investment and 50 workers**;
  **25% additional incentives** for thrust sectors and border areas; time-bound approvals under the
  **Right to Business Act** and single-window facilitation through **FastTrack Punjab** /
  **Invest Punjab**.
- **Three dedicated textile policies** notified by Punjab: **Spinning & Weaving**,
  **Dyeing & Finishing**, and **Apparel & Technical Textiles**.
- **Punjab Startup Policy 2025** — ₹3 L seed grants, ~8% loan interest subsidy, and a Startup Punjab
  Hub platform (relevant if you build a tech-enabled D2C tailoring product).
- **PSIDC / PSIEC** for industrial land and sheds (industrial land in Punjab reportedly
  ₹22–95 lakh/acre depending on location).
- **District Industries Centre (DIC), Jalandhar** — your first physical stop for PMEGP, subsidies and
  any state scheme. It's free and they will hand you the forms.
- **Three textile parks sanctioned in Punjab under SITP**: Ludhiana Integrated Textile Park, Lotus
  Integrated Tex Park, Rhythm Textile & Apparel Park — relevant when you manufacture.

> **Practical warning:** state incentives are almost always tied to *fixed capital investment* in a
> *manufacturing* unit in a *notified area*, with a defined employment commitment. A trading shop
> in Model Town will not qualify. **Don't structure your plan around a subsidy.** Structure it
> around cash flow, then take whatever subsidy turns out to apply.

## 8. Contracts and legal documents you should have

| Document | With | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Rent/lease agreement** (registered if > 11 months) | Landlord | 3-year lock-in with a 1-year exit clause; a rent-escalation cap (10%/yr max); a clause permitting signage and interior work; **the deposit refundable and documented** |
| **Supplier rate & supply agreement** | Ludhiana/Delhi/mill suppliers | Rates, MOQ, replacement, credit, delivery, dispute resolution |
| **Trade buyer agreement + credit policy acknowledgement** | Wholesale customers | Signed credit limit, payment terms, 18% late-payment interest, cheque-bounce consequences |
| **Employment/appointment letters** | All staff | Wage, hours, notice period, confidentiality of the customer database and measurement records |
| **Non-compete + customer-list confidentiality** | Master cutter, sales boys | Your customer list and your trade buyer list are your real assets. A salesman who leaves with them takes your business. Include it in the appointment letter — enforceability is limited but the deterrent is real |
| **Uniform/institutional supply contract** | Schools, hospitals | Specifications, quantity, delivery schedule, penalty, payment terms, force majeure |
| **Job-work agreement** | Outside tailors you outsource to in peak season | Rate per garment, quality standard, penalty for rework, fabric accountability (they must return offcuts) |
| **Franchise/agency agreement** | If you later take a brand agency or franchise out | Territory, minimum purchase, exclusivity, exit |
| **Partnership deed / shareholders' agreement** | If you bring in a partner | Profit share, capital, roles, exit valuation, deadlock resolution. Get a lawyer; ₹15,000 now saves a business later |

**Two specific legal risks in this trade:**
1. **Cheque bounce (Section 138 NI Act)** — your protection on trade credit. Take post-dated cheques
   from Slab C/D buyers, and send a statutory notice within 30 days of dishonour. Most disputes
   settle at the notice stage.
2. **Design/copyright** — don't lift another brand's printed designs or copy a competitor's
   brand name. If you create original prints/embroidery designs, register them
   (Designs Act, 2000) once they're commercially significant.

## 10. The 12 questions to ask your CA at the first meeting

1. Given my mix of fabric sales (goods) and stitching (services), what is my exact GST registration
   threshold, and should I register voluntarily now?
2. Should I be in the **regular** scheme or **composition**? What does each cost me per year?
3. When I bill "fabric + stitching" together, how should the invoice be structured to stay at 5%?
4. What happens when a stitched garment crosses **₹2,500 per piece** and becomes 18%? How do I
   price around that?
5. Am I eligible for **presumptive taxation** under 44AD/44ADA at the current rates, and is it
   better for me than regular books?
6. Which **accounting software** handles roll-wise fabric stock in metres plus GST plus e-way bills?
7. What is my **advance tax** liability this year, and on which dates?
8. How do I structure **TDS** on tailor salaries, on job-work payments to outside tailors, and on
   professional fees?
9. When do **EPF/ESI** become mandatory for me, and what will they cost per employee?
10. Should I stay a **proprietor** or become an **LLP/Pvt Ltd** — and at what revenue does the
    answer flip?
11. Which **loan scheme** (Mudra / PMEGP / Stand-Up India / PM Vishwakarma) fits my plan, and what
    project report format does the bank want?
12. What **insurance** must I have before I hold ₹10 L of fabric stock?

Get the answers **in writing** (even by WhatsApp) and file them.
