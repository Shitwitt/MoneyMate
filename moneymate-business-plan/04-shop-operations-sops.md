# 04 · Shop Operations — SOPs, Templates and Checklists

Copy these into a notebook, print them, or paste them into a Word file. A tailoring business
becomes a real business the day its quality stops depending on who is standing in the shop.

---

## SOP 1 — Taking an order (front desk, 12 minutes)

1. **Greet and seat.** Water. Lookbook on the table. Never take an order standing at the door.
2. **Ask the three questions before showing anything:**
   - *"Kaunse function ke liye banauna hai?"* (occasion)
   - *"Kado tak chahida hai?"* (date — write it down)
   - *"Budget kive rakhna chahunde o?"* (budget — say it out loud, it removes awkwardness)
3. **Offer three options in the customer's budget — low, mid, high.** People choose the middle.
   This is why you must *have* a middle.
4. **Show fabric** (your rolls or swatch book) even if they brought their own cloth. 30% will switch.
5. **Measure** (SOP 2) in the trial room, not in the shop aisle.
6. **Discuss style on paper** — draw or use a style sheet: lapel width, buttons, vents, pockets,
   lining, pleats, cuff style, monogram. Photograph a reference if the customer has one.
7. **Fill the order form** (template below), get the customer's signature on the *style column*.
   A signature on style ends 90% of "mainu eh nahi chahida si" arguments.
8. **Take 50% advance.** Give a printed receipt with order no., delivery date, and the balance.
9. **Book the first fitting date NOW**, on the spot, in the calendar. Never "we'll call you".
10. **Say the delivery date out loud and write it on the bill.** Then underline it.

## SOP 2 — Measurement protocol

**Rules**
- Measure over thin clothing; ask the customer to remove a jacket/hoodie.
- Tape parallel to the floor for all circumferences; snug but not tight; two fingers' ease where
  ease is intended.
- Customer stands naturally, arms relaxed, feet together, looking straight ahead. **Do not let them
  suck in the stomach** — say: *"Normal khade ho jao, saah chhadd ke."*
- Always measure **both sides** for shoulder and sleeve; record the larger if they differ, and note
  the difference.
- Write **cm and inches**. Never round to a "convenient" number.
- Record **posture faults** — this is what separates a ₹600 shirt from a ₹1,500 shirt.

**Men — full chart**
| Code | Point | How |
|---|---|---|
| NK | Neck | Around the base of the neck, 1 finger ease |
| CH | Chest | Fullest part, under arms, tape level |
| WS | Waist | Natural waist (navel level for men's trousers as worn) |
| HP | Hip/Seat | Fullest part of the seat |
| SH | Shoulder | Bone to bone across the back |
| SL | Sleeve length | Shoulder bone → wrist bone, arm slightly bent |
| SR | Sleeve round / bicep | Around the fullest part of the upper arm |
| WR | Wrist | Around the wrist bone + ease |
| JL | Jacket length | Base of neck (back) → desired length |
| TL | Trouser length | Waist → ankle bone, as worn |
| TH | Thigh | Fullest part of the thigh |
| KN | Knee | Around the knee |
| BO | Bottom opening | As per style (skinny 14", regular 16–17", classic 18–19") |
| CR | Crotch / rise | Waist front → through legs → waist back |
| FT | Front rise / back rise | Separately, for high-belly customers |
| **Posture** | stooped / erect / slope shoulder L-R / high-low hip / prominent seat / belly / chest | Write it |

**Ladies — full chart**
Bust · under-bust · waist · hip · shoulder · armhole (front/back) · sleeve length · sleeve round ·
bicep · wrist · kurta/top length · salwar length · neck depth front · neck depth back · bust point
to point · dart length · dupatta length · lehenga length + waistband · **figure notes**
(sloping shoulder, full bust, flat seat, sway back, maternity).

**Storage:** one numbered card per customer → scanned/photographed → stored in a Google Sheet or
your billing software. Columns: card no · name · phone · city · date · occasion · all measurements ·
style notes · order history · next reminder date.

> **This database is the most valuable thing you own.** 500 customers × ₹6,000 average ×
> one repeat every 2 years = ₹15 L of annual revenue you never have to advertise for.

## SOP 4 — Cutting discipline (where your fabric profit lives)

1. **Fabric inspection before cutting:** check shade continuity across the roll, weaving defects,
   stains, and **shrinkage** (a 3% shrink on 3.5 m = 10 cm lost). Pre-shrink/wash cottons.
2. **Record the metre reading** on the roll before and after cutting, on the job card. Stock theft
   in a fabric shop is almost always *unrecorded cutting*, not robbery.
3. **Nap / pattern direction:** all pieces same direction for pile fabrics (velvet, corduroy);
   match stripes and checks at centre back, shoulders, sleeves and pockets. A mismatched check
   is the fastest way to lose a premium customer.
4. **Marker efficiency:** lay all pieces of an order together; target ≥ 85% fabric utilisation.
   Every 5% you save on 1,000 m/month at ₹250/m = **₹12,500/month**.
5. **Keep end-pieces ≥ 30 cm separately** — they become pocketing, waistband facings, samples and
   swatches. Sell nothing under 30 cm as waste.
6. **Two-person rule for bespoke:** cutter cuts, owner verifies against the measurement card
   before the first stitch. Signature both.

## SOP 5 — Fittings (the trust moment)

- **Always by appointment.** A customer who waits 40 minutes at a fitting never books again.
- Garment is **basted, not finished** — nothing is cut away permanently at fitting 1.
- Pin and mark **on the body**, then write the changes on the job card in words and numbers.
- Take a **photo** of the pinned garment from front, side, back (with permission) — the tailor
  working on it may not be the one who fitted it.
- Ask: *"Baahein uthaa ke vekho. Baith ke vekho."* Movement tests catch what a mirror doesn't.
- **Never say "theek hai ji"** while pinning. Say what you are changing and why — that is what
  justifies the Signature price.
- Fit checks in order: shoulder line → chest ease → waist suppression → sleeve pitch & length →
  collar hug (no gap at the neck) → jacket balance (front not opening) → trouser waist/seat →
  break at the shoe → length of everything.

## SOP 6 — QC checklist (11 points, sign before packing)

| # | Check |
|---|---|
| 1 | Every measurement matches the card within ±0.5 cm |
| 2 | No puckering, no skipped stitches; SPI (stitches per inch) 10–12 for shirts, 8–10 for coats |
| 3 | Seams pressed open/flat and finished (overlock or bound) |
| 4 | Stripes/checks match at CB, shoulders, sleeves, pockets |
| 5 | Lining smooth, correct colour, no twist, adequate ease (sleeve & back) |
| 6 | Buttons: correct count, securely attached (shank on coats), buttonholes clean, no fraying |
| 7 | Zip runs smoothly, is the right length and colour, no fabric caught |
| 8 | Hems even and level all round; trouser hem has the correct break |
| 9 | No thread ends, no chalk marks, no pins, no stains, no iron shine |
| 10 | Final press/steam crisp; collar and lapel roll correct; creases sharp |
| 11 | Label, care card, spare buttons in a small envelope, garment bag clean |

Anything failed → back to the responsible stage, logged on the **rework board** with the reason.

## SOP 7 — Delivery and the follow-up

1. Customer tries it on **in the trial room** before paying. You stand outside the curtain.
2. If it needs a tweak: promise a date, do it free, deliver to their home if it's a Signature/Bespoke order.
3. Full payment, printed bill, garment bag, spare-button envelope, care card.
4. **Photo** with the customer's permission → Instagram/WhatsApp status.
5. **Review request on the spot** — phone in hand, Google link open. *"Do minute lagenge ji."*
6. **Add the next reminder date** to the database: wedding suit → shirt reminder in 3 months →
   blazer reminder next October → anniversary reminder yearly.
7. **Day-7 WhatsApp**: "Sir ji, suit kive lagya? Koi adjustment chahidi ho tan daso, free hai."
   This one message doubles your review count and halves your complaints.

## SOP 8 — Fabric retail counter (once you stock cloth)

1. **Greet within 10 seconds.** Fabric shoppers leave in 90 seconds if ignored.
2. **Ask the use before showing rolls:** suit / shirt / kurta / sherwani / uniform / ladies suit.
3. **Show 3 rolls, not 30.** Pull them out, unroll 1 m, drape it over the customer's arm.
   Fabric is sold by *touch and drape*, never by pointing at a rack.
4. **Take the customer to the mirror with the fabric against their skin tone.** Colour decisions
   are made at a mirror, not at a rack.
5. **Quote the "suit length pack" price first**, then the per-metre price. Anchoring works.
6. **Cross-sell every time**: lining, pocketing, buttons, zip, interlining, thread. Trims are
   55–70% margin and add ₹250–900 to a bill.
7. **Attach stitching**: *"Eh 3.5 m assi hi sil ke de dinde haan — 10 din, ₹X. Aaj fitting vi kar lainge."*
8. **Measure on the machine, cut in front of the customer**, and show them the reading. Trust in
   a fabric shop is entirely about believing the measurement.
9. **Bill every metre**, including 10 cm samples (bill at ₹0 or ₹20 — but record it).
10. **End of day**: reconcile roll readings against the day's bills. Any variance > 1 m = investigate
    the same night.

## SOP 9 — Inventory control (fabric)

- **Code every roll:** `CAT-BRAND/FIBRE-DESIGN-BATCH` e.g. `SUIT-PV-014-B23`.
- **Stock register columns:** code · supplier · GST invoice no · date · metres purchased ·
  landed rate/m · total cost · retail rate/m · opening metres · sold · balance · rack position.
- **Weekly physical count** of the top 20 rolls by value; monthly count of everything.
- **Reorder point:** when a roll falls below 15 m and it is a fast mover, reorder. When a design
  hasn't sold in 90 days, mark it **"clearance"** and move it — 20% off beats 100% dead.
- **Season rotation:**
  - Aug–Sep: stock up winter (wool, poly-wool, velvet, heavy jacquard)
  - Dec–Jan: stock up summer (linen, cotton, light suiting, chikankari base)
  - Feb–Mar: wedding/festive (silk, tissue, net, embroidery bases, sherwani fabric)
  - Jun–Jul: monsoon/rakhi/Diwali ethnic (Phulkari, bandhani, georgette, rayon)
- **Dead stock rule:** anything unsold for 2 seasons gets cut into **sample books and free swatches**
  for the trade network — converting dead capital into a sales tool.

## SOP 10 — Staff rules (write them, get them signed)

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Timings | 10:00–20:00, one 40-min lunch; Sunday half-day or rotation off |
| Late | 3 late arrivals in a month = half-day leave deducted |
| Mobile | Personal phones at the counter drawer during customer-facing hours |
| Dress | Clean shirt/uniform, trimmed nails, no strong smell of sweat or smoke |
| Cash | Only the owner/front-desk handles money; tailors never quote a price |
| Advances/salary loans | Up to ₹5,000, written, repaid over 3 months, one at a time |
| Rework | Rework caused by not following the job card is done in own time |
| Bonus | Piece-rate above target + Diwali bonus linked to declared annual profit |
| Leaving | 1 month notice; job cards and measurements belong to the shop |
| Safety | Machine guards on, no loose clothing near needles, first-aid box, fire extinguisher |
