# 10 · The Scoreboard, the Risks, and the Checklists

Analysis is only useful if it becomes a number you check and an action you take. This file has
three parts: the **scoreboard**, the **risk register**, and **print-and-use checklists**.

# PART 1 — The scoreboard

**The three questions that matter more than the table:**
1. *Which single number got worse this week, and why?*
2. *What is the one thing I will fix next week?*
3. *Did I pay myself a salary this month?*

**Three ratios to compute every month:**
- **Gross margin %** = gross profit ÷ revenue. Below 40% → your pricing or your buying is wrong.
- **Rent-to-revenue %**. Above 8% → you have the wrong shop or not enough traffic.
- **Labour-to-revenue %** (production labour). Above 35% in tailoring → productivity problem.

## 1.3 Quarterly strategic review (90 minutes, alone or with your partner)

1. Revenue by engine — which grew, which shrank?
2. Top 20 customers by revenue. Have any gone quiet? Call them this week.
3. Dead stock list. What gets cleared this quarter, and at what discount?
4. Trade buyer ageing report. Who gets cut off?
5. Team: who is underpaid relative to their output? Who is overpaid? Who is ready to promote?
6. Competition: visit 2 competitors. What did they change?
7. Capacity: are we refusing orders, or hunting for them? Which one is the problem?
8. One **strategic decision** per quarter — write it down with a deadline. Examples: raise prices;
   open the godown; hire the second cutter; launch own brand; drop the ladies' line.

# PART 2 — Risk register

| # | Risk | Prob. | Impact | What kills you | Prevention / mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dead fabric stock** | High | High | ₹6–15 L of capital frozen in rolls nobody wants | Buy depth not breadth; max 40 m per design until it sells once; 90-day clearance rule; season calendar |
| 2 | **Trade receivables blow up** | High | **Fatal** | 15% wholesale margin wiped out by 2–3 defaulters | Written credit policy before the first credit sale; slabs; auto-stop; PDCs; incentive on collections not sales; 1.5% monthly provision |
| 3 | **Master cutter leaves** | Medium | **Fatal** | Delivery stops; fit quality collapses | Train a second cutter from year 1; pay a % of bespoke revenue; a written non-solicit clause; document patterns/blocks digitally |
| 4 | **Late delivery in wedding season** | High | High | One angry groom's family = 50 lost customers | Never book above 110% of capacity; delivery calendar on the wall; say "no" out loud; rush fee to ration demand |
| 5 | **Fire in the fabric godown** | Low | **Fatal** | Total loss of stock in minutes | Fire extinguishers + smoke detection; no exposed wiring; no smoking; stock off the floor; **insurance at replacement value**; separate godown from retail |
| 6 | **Price war with Sadar Bazar / online** | High | Medium | You discount until you can't pay rent | Never compete on price. Compete on cloth+fit+guarantee+service, which they can't copy |
| 7 | **Seasonality cash crunch (Jul–Sep, Jan)** | High | Medium | You borrow at 18% to pay rent | Keep 2 months of fixed cost as cash from the season; use the off-season for uniforms and B2B; plan stock buying to season cash |
| 8 | **Owner burnout / one-man dependency** | High | High | You are the cutter, salesman, accountant and delivery boy | SOPs, a front-desk hire, weekly review, one full day off per week from year 2 |
| 9 | **Unrecorded fabric consumption by your own tailors** | Medium | Medium | 2–5% of stock disappears quietly | Every metre leaves a roll only against a job card; weekly roll reconciliation; CCTV at the cutting table |
| 10 | **GST/compliance default** | Medium | High | Penalties, blocked ITC, bank loan rejection | CA on a monthly retainer; return calendar on the wall; 100% invoicing from day 1 |
| 11 | **Cheap unregistered competition** | High | Medium | They quote 20% less because they pay no tax and no rent | Sell the guarantee, the invoice, the GST credit (B2B buyers need it), the trial room, the delivery date |
| 12 | **Fashion/assortment misjudgement** | Medium | Medium | You buy what you like, not what sells | Track every design's sell-through weekly; buy reorder-only for proven designs; clear losers in 90 days |
| 13 | **Credit given to retail walk-ins** | Medium | Medium | "Kal de jaanga" × 50 customers | Zero credit at retail. Printed on the counter. No relatives exception |
| 14 | **Bank refuses finance** | Medium | Medium | Growth stalls at Stage B | All sales through bank/UPI, 2 yrs ITR + GST returns, Udyam, CGTMSE cover, stock register, clean CIBIL |
| 15 | **Supplier shade variation / short measure** | Medium | Medium | A ruined premium suit, a lost customer | Signed & dated samples; 8-point inspection on arrival; one than per design per order; daylight colour check |
| 16 | **Staff theft (cash or cloth)** | Medium | Medium | Slow bleeding | Daily cash reconciliation, CCTV, roll readings, two-person rule on bespoke cutting, treat people well |
| 17 | **Family conflict over money/roles** | Medium | **Fatal** | The most common killer of Punjabi family businesses | Written roles, written profit share, a monthly family business meeting with minutes, separate personal and business accounts |
| 18 | **Over-expansion (2nd shop too early)** | Medium | **Fatal** | Cash spread thin, neither shop works | The 6-months-profitable-without-you rule |
| 19 | **Health/accident of the owner** | Low | High | The business *is* you | Term life insurance, health cover, a written "if I'm unavailable for 30 days" plan naming who does what |
| 20 | **Demand shift to ready-made / online** | Medium | Medium | Long-run structural | Your answer is exactly this plan: own the fit, own the cloth, own the occasion. Ready-made can't do bespoke; online can't do a fitting in Jalandhar |

## The four existential risks, ranked

1. **Trade receivables** (wholesale credit) — kills more textile businesses in Punjab than anything else.
2. **One-man dependency** — no second cutter, no SOPs, no systems.
3. **Family/ownership conflict** — no written agreement, no separate accounts.
4. **Dead stock + over-expansion together** — you buy a second shop to "move" the stock you couldn't sell in the first.

# PART 3 — The 25 mistakes (read before you spend a rupee)

**Pricing & positioning**
1. Competing on price instead of on fit, service and guarantee.
2. No printed price list — so every order is a negotiation and every negotiation is a discount.
3. No rush fee in wedding season — you subsidise bad planning with your own margin.
4. Discounting the premium tier instead of moving the customer to a lower tier.
5. Charging the same for a Tuesday shirt and a groom's sherwani.

**Fabric buying**
6. Buying 100 designs × 20 m instead of 30 designs × 40 m.
7. Buying stock before doing 8 weeks of swatch-book selling.
8. Not keeping a signed, dated sample of everything you ordered.
9. Ignoring width (44" vs 58") — it changes every suit's fabric requirement.
10. Paying cash without a GST invoice and losing 5% ITC forever.

**Operations**
11. No job card, so nobody knows where a garment is or who touched it.
12. Booking 130% of capacity and delivering late in wedding season.
13. Letting tailors take cloth from rolls without recording it.
14. No rework log — so the same mistake repeats 40 times a year.
15. Fittings by "call kar denge" instead of a booked appointment.

**Money**
16. Business cash in the personal savings account.
17. No salary for yourself, so you can't see real profit.
18. No cash reserve going into July–September.
19. Credit to retail customers.
20. No written credit policy before the first wholesale credit sale.

**Growth**
21. Opening a second shop before the first runs without you.
22. Hiring a 4th tailor without raising the average charge per garment.
23. No customer database — so every season starts from zero.
24. Treating marketing as "posting on Facebook sometimes" instead of a weekly system.
25. Trying to build the wholesale business before the retail fit reputation exists.

# PART 4 — Print-and-use checklists

## ✅ Checklist A — Launch week (do these 20 things before you open the fabric counter)

```
□ Rent agreement signed, registered, deposit receipted
□ Trade licence applied/received from MC Jalandhar
□ GST registration active; GSTIN on the board and the bill
□ Udyam registration certificate printed
□ Current account open; UPI QR at the counter
□ CA engaged; billing software live; roll-wise stock module tested
□ Shop insurance (fire + stock + burglary) policy in hand
□ Fire extinguisher mounted; CCTV live; wiring checked
□ All rolls coded, entered in the stock register, ticketed with price/m
□ Rate board printed and displayed (per metre + suit length + package)
□ 40 hero designs on hangers; 2 finished garments displayed with their fabric
□ Trial rooms done, mirrors clean, lighting tested at 6 pm
□ Measuring machine calibrated against a steel tape
□ Staff trained on: 10-step sale, 8 quality tests, GST billing, roll reconciliation
□ Order pads, measurement cards, job cards, delivery receipts printed
□ Google Business Profile updated ("Fabric store" category, 15 new photos)
□ Instagram: 9 posts before launch day
□ WhatsApp: soft-launch broadcast sent to the customer database
□ 10 vendor partners personally invited to the opening
□ First-30-days tracking sheet ready (m/day, conversion %, avg bill, attach %)
```

## ✅ Checklist B — Ludhiana/Delhi sourcing trip

```
BEFORE
□ Supplier spreadsheet from the last trip reviewed
□ Top-20 selling designs and dead designs identified from stock register
□ Cash/UPI limit decided; bank transfer ready
□ Tools packed: measuring tape, digital scale, lighter, notebook, camera, existing samples
□ List of 25 shops to visit with addresses and phone numbers
□ Empty dicky/boot space for thans

DURING (per shop)
□ Ask per-than AND per-metre rate
□ Ask composition, GSM, width, shrinkage %
□ Ask MOQ, mixed-design-than allowance, credit terms, cash discount
□ Ask replacement policy for shade variation/defect/shortage — get it in writing
□ Verify GSTIN
□ Photograph designs (with permission)
□ Take a physical swatch, labelled with the supplier's name and rate
□ Burn test one thread from a new supplier
□ Weigh a 10×10 cm swatch to compute GSM
□ Check shade continuity by unrolling 3–5 m in daylight
□ Collect the visiting card and note the contact person's name
□ Negotiate — quote a real competing rate; ask for the opening/distributor rate
□ Confirm freight terms and delivery to Jalandhar

AFTER (same night)
□ Spreadsheet updated with all 25 shops
□ Top 5 shortlisted; trial order placed with 2–3
□ GST invoices filed month-wise
□ Stock register entries made on arrival
□ 8-point quality inspection on every roll before it goes on the rack
□ Payment diary updated (due dates for each invoice)
```

## ✅ Checklist C — Wedding order (groom package)

```
□ Occasion, date and venue written on the order form
□ Three options shown in the customer's budget (low/mid/high)
□ Fabric selected from stock or swatch book; code and metres recorded
□ Full measurement card completed (both sides measured; posture noted)
□ Style sheet signed by the customer (lapel, buttons, vents, pockets, lining, monogram)
□ Delivery date written, underlined, and spoken out loud
□ Fitting 1 and Fitting 2 dates BOOKED in the calendar at order time
□ 50% advance received; printed receipt given
□ Job card created and stapled to the garment bundle
□ Fabric issued from stock against the job card; roll reading recorded
□ Cutter's signature before the first stitch
□ Fitting 1 done; notes written in words and numbers; photos taken
□ Corrections logged and assigned
□ Fitting 2 done; customer confirms
□ 11-point QC signed
□ Press, steam, garment bag, spare buttons envelope, care card
□ Full payment before the garment leaves
□ Photo with the customer; Google review requested on the spot
□ Database updated: next reminder date set (+3 months shirt, +9 months blazer)
□ Day-7 WhatsApp follow-up sent
□ Vendor partner commission paid (if referred)
```

## ✅ Checklist D — New wholesale (trade) buyer

```
□ Firm name, proprietor, address, phone, WhatsApp captured on the trade form
□ GSTIN verified on the GST portal (if claiming to be registered)
□ Trade type identified (tailor / boutique / retailer / institution)
□ Shop physically visited and photographed (yes, actually go)
□ Estimated monthly requirement noted
□ Rate card issued with a version number and date
□ Hanging sample book issued and recorded (asset — get it back if they leave)
□ First 2 orders CASH ONLY — no credit
□ Credit slab proposed and approved in writing by the owner
□ Credit policy acknowledgement signed by the buyer
□ PDCs / guarantor taken if Slab C or D
□ Route and call frequency assigned
□ Added to the ageing report from day one
□ First-month payment behaviour reviewed before the slab is confirmed
```

## ✅ Checklist E — New employee onboarding (day 1)

```
□ Appointment letter signed (wage, hours, notice, confidentiality of the customer list)
□ ID and address proof copied; two references called
□ Bank account and UPI details for salary
□ PF/ESI applicability explained (and enrolled if applicable)
□ Group personal accident policy added
□ Shop rules sheet given (timings, mobile, cash handling, dress, rework, bonus)
□ Safety briefing: machine guards, needles, iron/steam, fire extinguisher location, first aid
□ SOP manual issued; the 6 SOPs relevant to their role read out loud and demonstrated
□ Mentor assigned for the first 30 days
□ Their workstation named and their name added to the job-card signature list
□ 30/60/90-day review dates put in the calendar
□ Told explicitly, in writing, what "good" looks like in their role
```

## ✅ Checklist F — Season readiness (run this on 20 September and 20 October every year)

```
20 SEPTEMBER — stock
□ Winter/wedding fabric ordered and 80% received
□ Sherwani, velvet, heavy silk, jacquard range in place
□ Lining, canvas, trims, buttons, zips stocked to 2× normal
□ Packaging (garment bags, boxes, carry bags) ordered
□ Dead stock cleared at discount to free cash and rack space
□ Suppliers' peak-season delivery commitments confirmed in writing
□ Cash/credit line arranged for the stock build

20 OCTOBER — capacity & demand
□ Tailor capacity calculated for Nov–Dec; extra karigar identified
□ Two-shift plan drafted; overtime rate announced
□ Delivery calendar for Nov–Dec built with a hard "full by" date
□ New rate list printed and announced to the customer database
□ Rush fee announced
□ Groom/groomsmen/family packages printed and given to all 10 vendor partners
□ Google Business Profile hours updated for the season
□ Instagram content calendar for 8 weeks ready (12 reels shot in advance)
□ WhatsApp broadcast to the full database: "season booking open"
□ NRI customers contacted individually with the season dates
□ Advance-payment policy re-communicated to staff
□ Cash-handling and reconciliation routine tightened for high volume
```

## The last page — the five sentences to remember

1. **Sell fit, not stitching.** Stitching is a commodity; fit is a promise.
2. **Sell cloth with the fit.** That's the only way one customer visit earns two margins.
3. **Buy deep, not wide.** 30 proven designs beat 100 guesses.
4. **Cash from customers, credit to nobody retail, and a written limit for every trade buyer.**
5. **Build the second rung before you reach for the third.** The ladder is the strategy.
