# 01 · The Ladder — 10 Rungs a Custom-Tailoring Business Must Climb

> You asked for the "steps and leaders". The **ladder** below is the order of steps.
> The **leaders** — the businesses to study and copy — are in the last section of this file.

Most tailoring businesses never grow because they climb the ladder **out of order**: they buy
fabric stock before they have a fit reputation, or they hire tailors before they have a price
list, or they open a second shop before the first one runs without the owner. Each rung below
has a **"done when"** test. Do not move up until you pass it.

---

## Rung 1 — Fix the foundation (Weeks 1–4, cost ≈ ₹5,000)

The unglamorous rung. It is also the one that produces the biggest profit jump per rupee spent.

**1.1 The measurement system.** Create a printed/digital measurement card with **every** point you
will ever need (see `04-shop-operations-sops.md` for the full chart):

- Men: neck, chest, waist, hip, shoulder, sleeve length, sleeve round, bicep, wrist, jacket length,
  trouser length, thigh, knee, bottom opening, crotch/ rise, plus **posture notes**
  (stooped / erect / sloping shoulder / high-low shoulder / prominent seat / belly).
- Women: bust, under-bust, waist, hip, shoulder, armhole, sleeve, kurta length, salwar length,
  neck depth (front/back), dart placement, plus figure notes.

**Rules:** one card per customer, numbered, kept forever. Measure over thin clothing, with the
customer standing naturally, tape parallel to floor, two fingers' ease where ease is intended.
Write in **both** centimetres and inches — never "andaz".

**1.2 The price list.** Three tiers, printed, laminated, behind the counter, and given to the
customer. No negotiating from a mood. See `03-tailoring-economics.md`.

**1.3 The delivery promise.** Pick a number of days you can hit **95% of the time** and print it on
the bill: "Delivery in 10 working days." Then build a delivery register. Late delivery is the
number-one reason a customer never comes back — bigger than bad stitching, because bad stitching
is discovered at the fitting and late delivery is discovered on the wedding day.

**1.4 The daily money book.** Every rupee in, every rupee out, and **a salary line for yourself**.
If you don't pay yourself a salary on paper, you will spend the business's money and call it profit.

**Done when:** you can produce, in under 60 seconds, the full measurement of any customer from
the last 3 years, your printed price list, and last month's profit number.

## Rung 2 — Reposition: from "tailor shop" to "made-to-measure studio" (Weeks 3–8, ₹60,000–1.5 L)

Same skill, same machines, 2–3× the price. This is a **perception** upgrade, and perception is
built from physical things the customer can see.

| What the customer sees | Cheap tailor shop | Made-to-measure studio |
|---|---|---|
| Name | "Sharma Tailors" / "New Fashion Darzi" | A brand: e.g. **"SILAI & CO."**, "Doaba Bespoke", "The Fit House" |
| Board | Hand-painted flex, phone number, clutter | Clean backlit board, 1 name, 1 line: *Made to measure · Suits · Sherwanis · Shirts* |
| Entry | Fabric bundles on the floor, machine noise | Cloth rolls on wall racks, glass, warm light, no clutter |
| Seating | Plastic chair | Sofa + water + a lookbook folder on the table |
| Trial | Standing in the shop, mirror 4 ft away | **Trial room** with a full-length mirror and a step |
| Conversation | "Ki silna hai?" | "Kaunse function ke liye? Budget kitna rakhna hai? Main 3 options dikhata haan." |
| Bill | Hand-written parchi | Printed GST invoice with order no., delivery date, fabric code |
| Packaging | Polythene bag | Garment bag with your logo + a care card + a measuring tape as a gift |

**Concrete to-do list:**
1. Pick a name that is easy to say in Punjabi **and** easy to spell in English (NRIs and Instagram
   both matter). Check that the Instagram handle and Google Business Profile name are free.
2. Add a **trial room** (6×4 ft is enough) and a full-length mirror. ₹12,000–18,000.
3. Re-light the shop: 2 warm-white panels at the mirror, 1 bright white over the fabric display.
   Fabric looks 30% more expensive under good light. ₹8,000–15,000.
4. Move the machines **out of the customer's sight line** — a partition, a curtain, or a back room.
5. Print a **lookbook**: 20 photos of your own best work, A4 laminated pages in a folder. This one
   object raises your price more than anything else in this list.
6. Uniform for staff — even just a clean shirt with your logo embroidered (₹450 each).

**Done when:** a stranger walks in and asks "aapka rate kya hai?" — and you can quote your top tier
without apologising, and they accept it.

## Rung 3 — Productise and price (Weeks 6–12, ~₹0)

Turn "stitching" into **named products with prices**. Customers cannot compare you to the shop
next door if your product is different from theirs.

**The three-tier ladder (men's example — calibrate to your market):**

| Tier | What it is | Price band (Jalandhar 2026) | Who buys it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Essential** | Stitching only, customer's fabric, 1 fitting, standard finish | Shirt ₹500–700 · Trouser ₹600–900 · Kurta-pyjama ₹900–1,400 · 2-piece suit ₹2,500–3,500 | Daily customers, alterations, referrals |
| **Signature** | Stitching + premium trims (real horn buttons, Bemberg lining, fused canvas) + **2 fittings** + garment bag + 3-month free alteration | Shirt ₹1,000–1,500 · Suit ₹5,000–7,500 · Sherwani ₹6,000–12,000 | Grooms' families, professionals, officers |
| **Bespoke** | Your fabric (from your rolls) + hand-finished details + **3 fittings** + home/hotel visit for measurement + delivery in a wooden box + 1-year alteration | Suit ₹12,000–35,000 · Sherwani ₹25,000–1,50,000 | Grooms, NRIs, politicians, business families |

**The bundle trick (this is how the fabric business pays for itself):**

> "Sir, aap cloth bahar se laaoge to ₹3,500/m lenge. **Hamara cloth lo — ₹2,900/m, aur silai
> ₹1,500 sasti.** Total vich ₹2,000 bach jaande ne, te fitting di guarantee assi dinde haan."

You give a discount on stitching and recover it in fabric margin. The customer saves money, you
increase ticket size from ₹3,500 to ₹14,000, and your fabric stock starts turning.

**Pricing discipline — the three rules:**
1. **Never discount the Signature/Bespoke tier.** Discount the *scope* instead (" Essential tier vich
   kar dinde haan") — this protects your price integrity.
2. **Raise prices once a year, in October**, before the wedding season, by 10–12%. Announce it
   with a WhatsApp message to your customer list: "*1 November ton rate list update ho rahi hai —
   purane customers layi 25 October tak purane rate.*" This converts a price rise into a sales push.
3. **Rush fee = 30–50% extra, always.** Wedding-season urgency is the highest-margin money you
   will ever earn. If you don't charge it, you're subsidising people who planned badly.

**Done when:** ≥ 30% of your orders are Signature or Bespoke, and your average bill value has
doubled versus 3 months ago.

## Rung 4 — Build the fabric offer (Months 2–5, ₹4–10 L) — **your specific gap**

Full detail in [`06-fabric-business-guide.md`](06-fabric-business-guide.md). Summary of the rung:

1. **Two market trips before one rupee of stock.** Ludhiana first (closest, best for suiting and
   woolens), then Delhi (Gandhi Nagar / Chandni Chowk–Katra Neel) and, at volume, Surat.
2. **Start with 8 categories, ~1,290 metres, ~₹2.7 L of stock** (see stock plan in the financial
   model) — depth over breadth. No single design above 40 m until it has sold once.
3. **Buy swatch books / hanging samples first** (₹300–1,500 each). A swatch book lets you sell a
   fabric you don't stock, then order it. This is how you get sales without capital.
4. **Set up the retail mechanics:** roll racks, a measuring machine (yard counter), a cutting table,
   a rate board, GST-compliant billing, and a **fabric code** for every roll (Category-Design-Batch).
5. **Sell three ways:** by the metre, by the **suit length** (3.5 m pack with lining + buttons),
   and as a **cloth + stitching bundle**.

**Done when:** fabric is ≥ 35% of your monthly revenue, your stock turns ≥ 5×/year, and you can
answer "kaunsa navy suit chahiye?" without asking a supplier.

## Rung 5 — Build the demand engine (Months 2–12, ₹8,000–25,000/month)

Full detail in [`05-marketing-and-customers.md`](05-marketing-and-customers.md). The minimum viable engine:

- **Google Business Profile** — claimed, 10+ photos, category "Tailor" + "Fabric store",
  and 45+ reviews. This is your single highest-ROI asset in a city like Jalandhar.
- **Instagram** — 3 reels/week: before–after fit comparisons, fabric close-ups, cutting-room
  footage, "how to tell good suiting from bad". Punjabi + English captions. Reels of *fit
  transformations* travel in Punjab like nothing else.
- **WhatsApp Business** — catalogue, quick replies, broadcast lists segmented by
  (grooms / repeat customers / NRI / tailors-B2B). Status updates daily in season.
- **The customer database** — every measurement card gets a phone number, an occasion and a
  "next reminder date". A wedding suit customer needs a shirt 3 months later and a blazer next winter.
- **Vendor network** — makeup artists, photographers, tent/decor houses, banquet managers,
  wedding planners, pandits/gurdwara committees. 10% referral commission or a free garment.
- **NRI channel** — Jalandhar is Doaba's NRI heartland. Video-call measurement, couriered swatch
  box, 3-fitting plan compressed into their 3-week India visit. This is your highest-ticket segment.

**Done when:** at least 25% of new customers say "Instagram/Google te vekh ke aaye haan" and your
wedding-season order book is full **6 weeks before** the season starts.

## Rung 6 — Systemise the floor (Months 4–12)

The order flow, with a **job card** that is stapled to the garment and never removed:

```
Order book → Measurement card → Job card created
        ↓
   CUTTING (master cutter signs) ← fabric issue from stock (fabric code + metres recorded)
        ↓
   STITCHING (tailor name + start/finish date)
        ↓
   FIRST FITTING (customer present — notes written on the job card)
        ↓
   CORRECTION → SECOND FITTING → FINISHING (buttons, hem, thread trim)
        ↓
   QC CHECKLIST (11 points, signed) → PRESS/STEAM → PACK (garment bag + care card)
        ↓
   DELIVERY (customer signs, photo taken for Instagram, review requested)
```

**The three boards on your wall:**
1. **Order board** — every live order as a card in columns: New / Cutting / Stitching / Fitting /
   Finishing / Ready / Delivered. Nothing hides.
2. **Delivery calendar** — the next 21 days, with the delivery date of every order. Anything red
   gets attention today.
3. **Rework board** — every alteration, whose fault, why. Reviewed every Sunday. If one tailor
   appears 5 times, that's a training issue or a salary issue, not a customer issue.

**Done when:** the shop delivers correctly for 5 working days with you absent.

## Rung 7 — Team and skills (Months 6–24)

**The hierarchy you are building:**

| Role | Pays (Jalandhar 2026) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Master cutter** | ₹25,000–45,000 + % of bespoke revenue | The single most valuable person. Cutting decides 80% of fit. **Never let this role be a single point of failure** — train a second. |
| Head tailor (coat/suit specialist) | ₹20,000–32,000 | A proper 2-piece suit with canvas is a specialist skill, not a general one |
| Tailor (shirt/trouser/kurta) | ₹14,000–22,000 | Volume work |
| Ladies' specialist / boutique tailor | ₹15,000–25,000 | Blouse, lehenga, anarkali — highest-margin stitching per hour |
| Finisher + press | ₹9,000–14,000 | Perceived quality lives here: thread ends, crisp press, sharp crease |
| Fabric salesman/cutter | ₹12,000–18,000 + commission | Must be able to *sell*, not just measure |
| Front-desk / order manager | ₹12,000–18,000 | Takes the order, manages WhatsApp, chases delivery. Frees you to sell. |

**Pay structure that works:** fixed salary + **piece-rate bonus** for units above target +
**rework penalty** (not money — the rework is done in their own time) + a Diwali bonus tied to
annual profit. Publish the scheme; don't keep it in your head.

**Retention levers that cost nothing:** a machine of their own (not shared), a named workstation,
Sunday off, tea twice a day, their name on the job card, and one garment stitched free for them
each Diwali. Tailors leave for ₹1,500/month; they stay for respect and a working chair.

**Hiring sources:** Ludhiana and Hoshiarpur tailor networks, ITI/ Polytechnic garment departments,
Samarth-scheme trained candidates, and — the best source — your own apprentice pipeline
(hire a ₹7,000 helper, train for 18 months, promote).

**Done when:** no single employee leaving can stop delivery for more than 3 days.

## Rung 8 — Institutionalise (Months 6–18)

This rung is what turns a shop into a **business that a bank will fund and a buyer will value**.

1. **Current account** in the business name (not your savings account). Punjab National Bank /
   SBI / HDFC all have MSME desks; take the one whose branch manager knows your name.
2. **Udyam (MSME) registration** — free, online, gives you priority-sector lending, government
   tender eligibility, and protection under the MSME Samadhaan Act for delayed payments
   (huge in wholesale — buyers must pay in 45 days or face interest).
3. **GST registration** — mandatory above threshold, but register **voluntarily earlier**:
   B2B wholesale is impossible without a GST number, and tailoring is now at only **5%**.
4. **Shop & Establishment registration** (Punjab Shops & Commercial Establishments Act) and
   **municipal trade licence** from Jalandhar Municipal Corporation.
5. **Bookkeeping** — Tally/Zoho Books/Busy + a CA who files your GSTR-1/3B and ITR on time.
   Two years of clean returns is the price of a ₹50 L working-capital limit later.
6. **Insurance** — shop/fire policy covering fabric stock (fabric burns and fabric gets water
   damage), and health/accident cover for every employee.
7. **A monthly P&L and a weekly scoreboard** (`10-kpis-risks-checklists.md`).

**Done when:** a bank officer can understand your business from your paperwork alone.

## Rung 9 — B2B and wholesale (Year 2–3)

Full detail in [`07-wholesale-and-scale.md`](07-wholesale-and-scale.md). Four revenue lines, in order of difficulty:

1. **Uniform contracts** — schools, colleges, hospitals, hotels, factories, security agencies,
   banks, gyms. Predictable, annual, and paid by cheque. Start with 2 schools and one hospital.
   (Punjab has thousands of schools; a single 800-student school = ~1,600 shirts + 800 trousers a year.)
2. **Supply other tailors and boutiques** — sell them fabric at wholesale. You are their supplier
   and they are your overflow capacity. Give them a **rate card, credit limit and a delivery van route**.
3. **Wedding-party packages** — groom + 8 groomsmen + father + 2 grandfathers, all measured in
   one visit, one colour story. Ticket size ₹1.5–6 L. This is the single most profitable product
   in Punjab tailoring.
4. **Field sales + godown** — 2–3 sales boys on bikes with swatch bags covering Jalandhar,
   Kapurthala, Hoshiarpur, Nawanshahr, Phagwara, Nakodar, Kartarpur, Phillaur.

**Done when:** B2B is ≥ 40% of revenue and you have 45+ active repeat trade buyers.

## Rung 10 — Brand, multi-city, export (Year 3+)

1. **Your own fabric brand.** Once you move 3,000+ m/month, stop buying other people's brands.
   Commission designs from a Ludhiana/Surat mill, print your own selvedge label and packaging,
   and sell "your" suiting. Own-brand margin is 25–35% vs 12–18% for a distributed brand.
2. **Second showroom.** Chandigarh (Mohali) or Ludhiana for premium; or a **smaller studio +
   big godown** model in Jalandhar outskirts to cut rent.
3. **D2C online.** Instagram shop + a website with a "book a measurement at home" flow.
   Made-to-measure sells online surprisingly well *if* you solve measurement (video calls,
   a mailed measuring kit, or a home-visit network).
4. **NRI/diaspora program.** Brampton, Surrey, Southall, Melbourne, Dubai. One trip a year,
   40 appointments, ₹40 L of orders. Jalandhar's diaspora links are your unfair advantage.
5. **Export.** Punjab's textile exports are ~₹12,241 Cr and growing. Route: become a
   **merchant exporter** of stitched garments first (buy from your own unit + Ludhiana knitters),
   register with the **Apparel Export Promotion Council (AEPC)**, use **RoSCTL/RoDTEP** rebates,
   and start with UAE/UK/Canada buyers who serve the same diaspora.
6. **Manufacturing unit.** Only when your retail + wholesale demand exceeds what job-work can
   supply. Then look at Punjab's **Industrial & Business Development Policy 2026** incentives
   (capital subsidy up to 20% of fixed capital investment capped at ₹10 Cr, up to 75% net SGST
   reimbursement, 100% electricity-duty exemption, total incentives up to 125% of FCI), and
   the SITP parks sanctioned in Punjab (Ludhiana Integrated Textile Park, Lotus Integrated Tex
   Park, Rhythm Textile & Apparel Park).

## The leaders to study (who to copy, and what exactly to copy)

**Copy the *system*, not the logo.**

| Leader | What to copy | What to ignore |
|---|---|---|
| **Raymond "Made to Measure"** | The **store ritual**: fashion adviser → need → fabric swatches → style options → master tailor measures → try-on. Their price architecture (a shirt at ~₹1,600 up to a 2-piece suit at ~₹15,000+ in earlier years; far higher now) shows what "consultation + fabric + fit" is worth. Also their **franchise/hinterland push** into tier-4/5 towns — proof that premium tailoring works outside metros. | Their capital intensity. You don't need a flagship; you need the ritual. |
| **The Raymond Shop franchisees** (e.g. the Bengaluru franchisee who started at ₹50,000 and reached ₹22 Cr turnover) | The **distribution logic**: take a brand, own a locality, expand the square footage as revenue grows, and add Made-to-Measure on top of ready-to-wear. Also: **sell the upper floors to fund the showroom.** | Assuming a franchise is cheaper than building your own name — it isn't, in the long run. |
| **Peter England / Van Heusen / Louis Philippe (Aditya Birla Fashion)** | **Fabric-first retail** — Linen Club-style fabric walls where the cloth is the hero; **season-wise collection drops**; **in-store tailoring tie-ups** inside their own showrooms. | Their advertising spends. |
| **Blackbuck / Arvind / Raymond suiting & shirting distributors in Ludhiana** | How a **rate book, sample book and a monthly van route** turns 200 small tailors into a distribution network. Study their credit discipline. | Their dependence on a single principal brand. |
| **Fabindia / Manyavar** | **Category ownership of an occasion.** Manyayar owns "the Indian wedding groom". You can own "the Punjabi wedding suit + sherwani in Doaba". Occasion ownership beats product ownership. | Their national-scale supply chain. |
| **Local heroes: the big Ludhiana woollen/knitwear exporters** | Punjab produces ~95% of India's woollen knitwear — these firms show how to convert a local cluster into export orders via AEPC, trade fairs and buyer-seller meets. | The export compliance burden in year 1. |
| **Online made-to-measure (e.g. Tailorman, Bivolino, SuitLTD, Indian MTM D2C brands)** | **Measurement capture at distance** — mailed measuring kits, video measurement, body-photo apps. This is the exact machinery you need for the NRI channel. | Their unit economics without a physical fitting. |
| **Your own city's best shop** | Find the one tailor in Jalandhar whose customers travel from Ludhiana and Amritsar. Buy a shirt from him. Note the packaging, the bill, the greeting, the mirror, the trial room, and the follow-up call. **Then match it and add fabric.** | — |

**The three people to hire/learn from, in priority order:**
1. A **master cutter** who has cut coats for a wedding shop (not just shirts).
2. A **fabric salesman** who has sold suiting on a wholesale route — he brings a buyer list.
3. A **CA who knows textiles** — GST on apparel changed in Sep 2025 and inverted-duty/ITC issues
   are real in this sector.

## The rung most people skip — and why it kills them

They skip **Rung 3 (productise and price)** and jump straight to **Rung 4 (buy cloth)**.

Result: a shop with ₹6 L of fabric stock, no price ladder, no fit reputation and no marketing,
competing on price with Sadar Bazar. The fabric sits. The tailors idle. The owner discounts
stitching to move cloth, which trains customers to expect discounts, which destroys the margin
that was supposed to pay for the fabric. Eighteen months later he sells the stock at cost and
goes back to being a tailor.

**Order matters: fit first, price second, cloth third, marketing fourth, scale fifth.**
