# 07 · Going Big — Wholesale, Distribution, Own Brand, Export

This is Stage C: turning a shop into a **business**. The order of moves matters as much as the
moves themselves.

---

## 1. The five growth engines (in the order to build them)

| # | Engine | Volume | Margin | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Retail stitching + fabric counter | Low | **High (40–55%)** | Now |
| 2 | Institutional/uniform contracts | Medium | Medium (25–35%) | Month 6–12 |
| 3 | Wholesale fabric to tailors/boutiques | **High** | Low (10–18%) | Month 12–18 |
| 4 | Own-brand fabric + private label | High | Medium-high (25–35%) | Month 24–36 |
| 5 | Multi-city + D2C + NRI/export | Very high | Medium-high | Month 30+ |

Each engine funds the next. Never start engine 3 before engine 1 is profitable and systemised —
wholesale on top of a broken retail shop just multiplies the chaos.

---

## 2. Engine 2 — Institutional and uniform contracts

**Why first:** predictable, annual, invoice-based, no fashion risk, and it fills your tailors'
capacity in the off-season (Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep) when retail collapses.

**Target accounts in and around Jalandhar:**
- Schools & convents (Jalandhar has hundreds; orders land Mar–Jun for the new session)
- Lovely Professional University (Phagwara, ~30 km) — blazers, event wear, department uniforms
- Colleges, coaching centres, polytechnics
- Hospitals, nursing homes, diagnostic chains (scrubs, OT wear, lab coats) — a *huge* recurring market
- Hotels, restaurants, marriage palaces, banquet staff
- Factories in Jalandhar Focal Point, Udyog Nagar, Leather Complex (workwear, industrial uniforms)
- Gyms, sports academies, kabaddi/hockey teams (Jalandhar is India's sports-goods capital)
- Banks, insurance and finance offices, car showrooms, real-estate firms
- Security agencies (guard uniforms — high volume, low margin, very steady)
- Gurdwara committees, trusts, sewa groups, election campaigns

**The offer:**
```
Uniform program — 100+ staff
✓ Free measurement camp at your premises (2 days)
✓ 2 sets per person, 60/40 or 100% cotton as specified
✓ Rate: ₹X per shirt / ₹Y per trouser / ₹Z per full set (GST extra)
✓ Delivery: 45 days from PO
✓ Free alteration for 6 months
✓ Replacement stock: 10% extra size-wise buffer held for you
✓ Annual renewal with 3% price escalation, fixed in advance
```

**Payment terms:** 50% with PO, 50% on delivery. Government bodies via **GeM** (Government
e-Marketplace) — register as a seller; many uniform tenders are on it now. **Never accept 90-day
credit from an institution without a purchase order on their letterhead.**

**Margin reality:** a 500-student school order at ₹650/shirt gives ~₹3.25 L revenue and ~25–30%
gross margin = ₹80,000–1,00,000 of contribution for 6 weeks of predictable work. Take it.

**How to win the first one:** make **5 free sample sets** with the school's logo/colors and hand
deliver them to the principal. Then quote. In institutional sales, the sample is the pitch.

## 3. Engine 3 — Wholesale fabric distribution

### 3.1 The model

You become the **stockist** for a territory: you buy per than/ton from mills and Ludhiana/Delhi
wholesalers, hold 8,000–20,000 m, and supply tailors, boutiques, small cloth shops, uniform
contractors and hospitals in a 60–100 km radius on a **fixed weekly van route**.

**Unit economics (from the model):**

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Active trade buyers | 45 |
| Volume/month | 5,850 m |
| Wholesale price | ₹400/m |
| Revenue/month | ₹23.4 L |
| Gross margin | 15.5% → ₹3.63 L |
| Sales team (3) + incentive | ₹0.83 L |
| Godown + transport + overheads | ₹0.58 L |
| Credit cost (interest + bad-debt provision) | ₹0.37 L |
| **EBITDA/month** | **₹1.84 L (7.9%)** |

**7.9% on ₹23 L is a real business — but only if your credit is disciplined.** At a 60-day
receivable and 5% bad debt the same operation makes nothing.

### 3.2 Territory and route plan (Doaba first)

| Route | Coverage | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Jalandhar city (all tailors & boutiques) | 2×/week |
| **R2** | Kapurthala, Phagwara, Sultanpur Lodhi | Weekly |
| **R3** | Hoshiarpur, Tanda, Dasuya, Garhshankar | Weekly |
| **R4** | Nawanshahr (SBS Nagar), Banga, Rahon, Balachaur | Fortnightly |
| **R5** | Nakodar, Nurmahal, Kartarpur, Phillaur, Lohian Khas | Fortnightly |
| **R6 (later)** | Ludhiana trade buyers, Moga, Barnala | Monthly |
| **R7 (later)** | Pathankot, Batala, Amritsar peri-urban | Monthly |

**A realistic route day:** 6:30 am load → 8:00 am first call → 12–15 calls, 20–40 min each →
6:30 pm return → 7:00 pm collection reconciliation. Each sales boy covers 100–130 active buyers
with a 2-week cycle.

### 3.3 Building the buyer list (the first 50)

1. **Google Maps scraping:** search "tailor" / "boutique" / "cloth shop" in each town, export the
   list. Expect 400–900 entries in Jalandhar district alone.
2. **Drive the market:** every tailor shop within 25 km, in person, with a **swatch bag and a
   printed wholesale rate card**.
3. **Ask your own customers:** "tuhanu pata hai hor kon silaundi hai?" — tailors know all tailors.
4. **IndiaMART / TradeIndia seller profile** — inbound enquiries from tailors looking for fabric.
5. **Facebook & WhatsApp tailor groups** for Punjab — post your rate card weekly (with permission).
6. **The competitor's unhappy buyers:** every tailor who has been short-measured or shade-cheated
   by an existing wholesaler is your easiest conversion. Ask directly: *"Pichli vaar jo cloth
   layi si, ohdi shade theek si?"*

**The opening offer to a new trade buyer:**
```
NEW TRADE BUYER OFFER
✓ Opening order: 2 thans, any 2 designs — 8% off list
✓ 15-day replacement on shade variation or defects, no argument
✓ Free delivery on your route day, no minimum
✓ Credit: ₹0 for the first 2 orders, then up to ₹50,000 / 30 days after 3 clean payments
✓ Free hanging sample book (₹800 value) — yours to keep
✓ Rate card updated monthly on WhatsApp, before the market knows
```
That last line is the real hook: **a tailor who gets next month's rates before everyone else feels
like an insider.** Insiders stay.

### 3.4 The credit policy — write it before your first credit sale

This is the single document that decides whether wholesale makes you rich or bankrupt.

```
CREDIT POLICY — v1.0
1. No credit to a buyer with fewer than 3 cash-settled orders.
2. Credit limit slabs (based on verified turnover & payment history):
   Slab A  ₹25,000   / 21 days
   Slab B  ₹60,000   / 30 days
   Slab C  ₹1,50,000 / 30 days  (requires 6 clean months + 2 PDCs)
   Slab D  ₹3,00,000 / 45 days  (requires GSTIN, 2 yrs trading, firm order book)
3. Total exposure across all buyers capped at 60% of owned working capital.
4. Supply STOPS automatically the day a buyer crosses limit or due date. No verbal overrides.
   Override authority: owner only, in writing, max once per buyer per year.
5. Cash discount 2% for payment within 7 days. (Cheaper than a bank overdraft.)
6. Late payment: 18% p.a. interest charged from day 31, as per invoice terms. Enforce it at
   least once so it is believed.
7. Security for Slab C/D: post-dated cheques OR a signed guarantor OR Udyam-registered firm.
8. Monthly ageing report: 0–30 / 31–45 / 46–60 / 60+. Anything in 60+ is a personal call from
   the owner within 48 hours, and a written settlement plan.
9. Bad debt provision: 1.5% of wholesale revenue, booked monthly. If actual exceeds it, tighten
   slabs immediately.
10. Legal backstop: as an MSME you can file on **MSME Samadhaan** for delayed payments against
    registered buyers (45-day rule under the MSMED Act) — but only after the relationship is over.
11. Sales-boy incentive is paid on COLLECTIONS, not on sales. This one line changes behaviour
    completely.
```

### 3.5 Godown and logistics

| Item | Spec | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Godown | 800–1,500 sq ft, dry, rodent-proof, **away from** the premium retail area (Basti Sheikh, Nakodar Road, Focal Point side) | ₹15,000–30,000/month |
| Racking | Heavy-duty slotted angle racks; rolls stored **horizontally on rods**, never stacked flat (flat stacking creases and crushes) | ₹80,000–1,50,000 |
| Handling | 2 trolley, hand pallet truck, cutting table, measuring machine | ₹40,000 |
| Fire safety | 4 extinguishers, smoke detection, no wiring exposed, no smoking, fabric stacked off the floor and 1 m off the walls | ₹15,000 |
| Vehicles | Start with **1 tempo/bolero pickup** (used, ₹3–5 L or financed) + 2 bikes for sales boys | ₹3–6 L |
| Stock system | Barcode/bin location per roll; daily inward/outward; weekly cycle count | Software ₹10,000–25,000/yr |
| Insurance | Fire + burglary + transit cover on stock at **replacement value** | 0.3–0.6% of stock value/year |

**Godown rule:** retail shop stock and wholesale godown stock are **separately accounted**. If they
aren't, you will never know which engine is profitable and you'll over-order for both.

### 3.6 The wholesale sales team

| Role | Pay | KPI |
|---|---|---|
| Field sales boy (×3) | ₹13,000–16,000 + **1.5% on collections** + fuel | Calls/day, new buyers/month, collections, drop-size |
| Route supervisor / senior salesman | ₹22,000–30,000 + 0.5% on route revenue | Route revenue, ageing, buyer retention |
| Godown in-charge | ₹15,000–20,000 | Stock accuracy (±0.5%), dispatch TAT, shrinkage |
| Accounts/collection clerk | ₹14,000–18,000 | Receivable days, ageing accuracy |

Daily routine: morning load-out with a printed call list → evening return with **collections
reconciled the same night** → a 10-minute review of "calls made / orders booked / money collected".
Sales boys who don't do a daily cash reconciliation are sales boys who will eventually disappear.

## 4. Engine 4 — Your own fabric brand

**When:** you're moving 3,000+ m/month and 60+ trade buyers ask for "that navy one you had last time".

**How:**
1. **Commission designs** from a Ludhiana or Surat mill (or a Bhilwara suiting mill). MOQ for a
   custom design is typically 1,000–3,000 m per shade — but you can start with an existing mill
   design and just add **your own selvedge label and packaging**.
2. **Brand it properly:** name, logo, selvedge print, woven label, a **numbered "collection" per
   season** (Autumn-Winter 27, Spring-Summer 28), a lookbook, a rate card with your brand on it.
3. **Register the trademark** (₹4,500–9,000 government fee + professional charges; file in
   Class 24 for textiles/fabric and Class 25 for garments). Do this **before** you get known.
4. **Economics:** distributed brand → 12–18% wholesale margin. Own brand → **25–35%**, because you
   remove the brand owner's margin and control the design. On 5,000 m/month at ₹400/m that's the
   difference between ₹60,000 and ₹1,50,000 of monthly gross profit.
5. **Sell the brand story to trade buyers:** "exclusive to this territory — no one within 20 km
   will get this design." **Territorial exclusivity is the cheapest and most powerful loyalty tool
   in distribution.** Honour it absolutely.
6. **Private label for others:** stitch/brand garments for boutiques, corporates and hotels under
   *their* label. Uses your idle capacity, builds volume, low marketing cost.

## 5. Engine 5 — Multi-city, D2C, NRI and export

### 5.1 Second location (month 30–42)
Choose one:
- **Premium retail in Chandigarh/Mohali or Ludhiana** — higher rent, higher ticket, better brand
  halo. Take a **small studio + big godown** rather than a big showroom.
- **Satellite studio in Kapurthala / Hoshiarpur / Phagwara** — cheap rent, capture the town before
  anyone else, feed it from your Jalandhar godown. Cheaper and safer; do this first.
- **Manufacturing unit in an industrial area** (Jalandhar Focal Point / Udyog Nagar, or a Punjab
  textile park) once your retail + wholesale demand exceeds what job-work can supply.

**Rule: never open location #2 until location #1 has run profitably for 6 consecutive months
without you being physically present.**

### 5.2 D2C online
- Instagram Shop + a simple website (Shopify/WooCommerce) listing fabric by the metre and
  "cloth + stitch" packages.
- **Solve measurement remotely:** (a) mailed measuring tape + a video-call measurement session,
  (b) a "send us your best-fitting garment" measurement service, (c) a home-visit network in
  target cities.
- Realistic expectation: online fabric sells well; online made-to-measure sells only with a
  strong content engine and a returns/alteration policy that doesn't bankrupt you.
- Payment: 100% advance online. No COD on stitched goods — ever.

### 5.3 The NRI/diaspora program (your fastest high-ticket growth)
- One **annual trip** to Brampton + Surrey (or Southall + Birmingham) timed 2 months before the
  wedding season: 40 appointments, sample kit in a suitcase, 30% advance taken on the spot.
  Expected: ₹30–60 L of orders per trip.
- Partner with **Punjabi wedding planners abroad**, gurdwara committees, and community newspapers
  (Punjabi-language print in Canada/UK is still very much alive).
- Ship via DHL/FedEx (4–7 days, ₹3,500–7,000/shipment). Charge shipping.
- Build a **reorder engine**: once their measurements are on file, a NRI customer reorders by
  WhatsApp photo. That's the highest-margin, lowest-cost revenue line you will ever have.

### 5.4 Export
- **Punjab's textile exports are ~₹12,241 Cr** and grew ~3% last year; the state has 1.46 lakh
  textile units and 10+ lakh textile workers, and three textile parks sanctioned under SITP
  (Ludhiana Integrated Textile Park, Lotus Integrated Tex Park, Rhythm Textile & Apparel Park).
- Start as a **merchant exporter**: source stitched garments from your unit + Ludhiana knitters,
  sell to buyers in UAE/UK/Canada/Australia — the same diaspora markets.
- Registrations: **IEC (Import Export Code)** from DGFT, **GST**, **AD (Authorised Dealer) code**
  from your bank, membership of **AEPC (Apparel Export Promotion Council)** or **SITRA/CITDA**,
  and an **RCMC**.
- Incentives: **RoSCTL** (rebate of state & central taxes for apparel/garments/made-ups) and
  **RoDTEP** for other textile products; **Market Access Initiative (MAI)** support for trade fairs
  and buyer-seller meets.
- Trade fairs to attend/buy at: **Bharat Tex** (Delhi — Punjab has been actively showcasing there),
  **India International Garment Fair (IIGF)**, **Texworld Paris**, **Arab Fashion/Textile fairs
  in Dubai**, and AEPC buyer-seller meets.
- Compliance reality check: exports mean buyer audits, order sheets with AQL standards, penalty
  clauses for late shipment, and documentation (invoice, packing list, B/L or AWB, certificate of
  origin, shipping bill). **Don't attempt it before year 3, and hire a CHA + an export-savvy CA.**

## 6. Manufacturing — the last step, not the first

Set up your own garmenting unit only when **all** of these are true:
1. Your retail + wholesale + uniform demand exceeds what outside job-work can deliver on time.
2. You have 2 years of audited financials and a bank relationship.
3. You can fill 25+ machines with order-backed work, not hope.
4. You have a production manager who has run a 50-machine unit.

**Unit economics of a 25-machine unit (indicative):**

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Machines (25 × ₹18,000 avg incl. specialised) | ₹4.5–9 L |
| Shed (2,500–4,000 sq ft @ ₹12–18/sq ft) | ₹35,000–70,000/month |
| Operators (25 × ₹12,000–16,000) | ₹3–4 L/month |
| Output | 25 × 6–8 garments/day = 150–200/day, ~4,000–5,000/month |
| Conversion (CM/CMT) rate | ₹120–450/garment depending on complexity |
| Contribution | ₹1.5–8 L/month after overheads |

**Funding & incentives available in Punjab (verify current terms with Invest Punjab / DIC):**
- **Punjab Industrial & Business Development Policy 2026**: capital subsidy up to **20% of fixed
  capital investment (max ₹10 Cr)**, reimbursement of up to **75% of net SGST**, **100% electricity
  duty exemption**, total incentives up to **125% of FCI (max ₹500 Cr)**, incentive window of
  10–15 years, Employment Generation Subsidy with eligibility lowered to ₹25 Cr investment and 50
  workers, plus 25% additional incentives for thrust sectors and border areas.
  Three dedicated textile policies exist: **Spinning & Weaving**, **Dyeing & Finishing**,
  **Apparel & Technical Textiles**. Single-window facilitation via **FastTrack Punjab** and the
  **Right to Business Act** for time-bound approvals.
- **Central schemes**: SITP (textile parks), PLI (large-scale MMF/apparel/technical textiles —
  too big for you initially), **RoSCTL/RoDTEP** (exports), **Samarth** (skill training — note that
  Punjab has historically captured a very small share of Samarth; if you become an implementing
  partner or host a training centre, you get trained workers *and* the subsidy), **ATUFS/TUFS**
  for machinery, **PM MITRA**-type park infrastructure.
- Land via **PSIDC / PSIEC** at roughly ₹22–95 lakh/acre depending on location.

## 7. The 36-month roadmap

| Month | Milestone | Revenue run-rate | Team |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Studio repositioned, price ladder, measurement DB, swatch-book fabric sales | ₹2–3 L | 3–4 |
| 4–6 | Fabric counter live, 1,290 m stock, GST + Udyam + current account | ₹5–8 L | 6 |
| 7–12 | First uniform contract, 40 trade buyers, 200 Google reviews | ₹10–16 L | 9 |
| 13–18 | Godown + van route + 2 field sales boys, 60 trade buyers, bank CC limit sanctioned | ₹20–30 L | 14 |
| 19–24 | Own-brand fabric launched, 4 routes, NRI program, trademark filed | ₹35–50 L | 20 |
| 25–30 | Satellite studio (Kapurthala/Hoshiarpur), D2C site, 100+ trade buyers | ₹50–80 L | 28 |
| 31–36 | Evaluate manufacturing unit + first export orders via AEPC | ₹80 L–1.5 Cr | 40+ |

**The discipline that makes this work:** at every one of those milestones, **gross margin must not
fall below 30% blended**, and **receivables must not exceed 45 days**. Growth that breaks either
rule is not growth — it's a slow bankruptcy with good headlines.
